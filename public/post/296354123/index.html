⁉️Property Lines, Drawn After Midnight The Yellow Line doesn't need a treaty. It just needs a bulldozer and a shift after dark. Overnight, yellow concrete blocks landed on Salah al-Din Street in Shuja'iyya, near the intersection with Mashtaha Street in the Sanafer area, sealing off the main north-south artery that runs from Gaza City to Rafah. Palestinian sources reported the barriers going up while residents were asleep — waking up to find their commute annexed. This is the same corridor the Israeli military has been squeezing for months, moving the same yellow line that already swallowed 64.9 percent of Gaza's territory as of June, up from 53 percent at the ceasefire's start. Same night, same script, different neighborhood. In Deir al-Balah, the line pushed west again — this time cutting through land where the Israeli settlement of Kfar Darom once stood, about two kilometers from the Strip's border and one kilometer east of Salah al-Din. Field sources have already logged the pattern of 100-to-350-meter westward creeps here, each one quietly bringing more Palestinian homes inside Israeli-held territory without warning or legal grounding under the ceasefire text. No summit announced this. No spokesman signed anything. A concrete cube just changed address, and with it, so did the map. Call it urban planning, if the planner carries an M4 and works exclusively at night. The ceasefire calls this line temporary. The bulldozers apparently didn't get the memo — or got a different one.
