⏰ OKUMA MOLASI ⏰ The Streisand Effect is a phenomenon in which attempts to suppress or remove information unintentionally increase public awareness of it. The term originated in 2003 after Barbra Streisand sought to limit the distribution of a photograph of her residence. Before the legal action, the image had attracted little attention. Afterward, media coverage dramatically increased public interest in it. The effect occurs because efforts at censorship often become newsworthy themselves. People may grow curious about the information being restricted and actively seek it out. In the digital age, social media and online sharing can amplify this process, allowing content to spread rapidly across networks. The Streisand Effect illustrates a broader principle of communication: controlling information is often more difficult than anticipated. In some cases, attempts at suppression may achieve the opposite of their intended goal.
