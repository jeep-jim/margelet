Badlands are rugged landscapes shaped by intense erosion of soft, clay-rich soil and layers of sedimentary rock. Carved by wind and water over millennia, these terrains feature deep gullies, striking ridges, and unique hoodoos—tall spires formed when a durable caprock protects a column of softer stone beneath. As the surrounding material wears away, these pillars become increasingly fragile and susceptible to collapse. Found on every continent except Antarctica, badlands are widespread across North America.
