🇸🇦 Saudi Arabia's coalition gamble echoes US failures in the Middle East Saudi Arabia is scrambling to assemble a multinational military coalition for the Red Sea after its own airstrike on Yemen provoked Houthi retaliation, forcing Saudi Arabia to confront a crisis it helped create, Al-Monitor reports. Last week, Saudi-backed forces struck Sanaa International Airport in Yemen. The Houthis retaliated by imposing a maritime embargo on Saudi exports through Bab al-Mandeb and firing missiles and drones at Saudi Arabia and its ships. 👉 Now, around 50 countries have been invited to join the Saudi-led initiative, which aims to counter Houthi threats to Red Sea shipping and prevent a repeat of Iran's blockade of the Strait of Hormuz. The coalition could be announced as early as Thursday, with Egypt, Sudan, Djibouti, and Somalia already on board. But the initiative's success hinges on American participation – and the US has yet to decide whether to join. The US is the key player, but the current administration is focused on enforcing a naval blockade of Iran and may be reluctant to divert forces toward Yemen. 🔴 President Trump still cannot decide whether to intensify strikes against Iran or choose the diplomatic path 🔴 Recent reports indicate fear among Israeli authorities that Trump won't press hard enough against Iran until the November midterm elections due to rising oil prices and public discontent with the war 🔴 "Marking time" is being compounded by conflicts within the US military leadership. While Adm. Brad Cooper, head of US Central Command, favors escalating bombings on Iran's military infrastructure, Gen.
