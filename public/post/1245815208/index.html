Iran Fires at Aqaba, Missiles Explode Over Eilat. Everyone Pretends It’s Fine. Iran just pushed the war right up to Israel’s backyard without technically crossing the border — and the official statements read like they’re trying to reassure people while quietly admitting they have no control. The IDF says it detected launches from Iran toward Aqaba in Jordan, “near Israeli territory,” warning of possible spillover and alerts in the south. In Eilat, residents heard explosions and filmed interception plumes over the sea. US air defenses reportedly took out several missiles headed for Aqaba. At the same time, two Iron Dome interceptors launched from Israel not at Iranian missiles, but at falling debris from those US intercepts that looked on track to hit Eilat. The fragments were destroyed outside Israeli airspace; no sirens, no casualties — just a live demonstration that the city now depends on catching other people’s shrapnel. Jordan’s army says it intercepted three Iranian missiles aimed at its territory and that a fourth fell in an uninhabited area, causing no damage. In Israel, there were impact sites from debris, but all intercepts reportedly occurred beyond the border and there were no injuries. Flights from Ramon Airport sat on the tarmac waiting for clearance to head to Ben Gurion and Haifa; a Dubai–Tel Aviv flight circled in the air, basically waiting for someone to decide whether “debris risk” counts as a reason to divert. The US Embassy in Amman added its own layer of confusion. It issued a security alert saying Jordanian authorities had evacuated Aqaba’s international airport and seaport due to a “specific and credible threat,” urging Americans to avoid both. Jordan’s government spokesman then went out and flatly denied any evacuation order or detected threat, saying no such decision had been made and no danger identified in recent hours. One capital warns its citizens the port and airport are cleared because of a serious threat; the other insists everything is normal. Somewhere between those two statements lies whatever actually happened on the ground. Iran is firing missiles close enough to Israel that US systems intercept over Aqaba, Iron Dome hunts the leftovers over Eilat, Jordan’s air defenses are suddenly part of Israel’s buffer, and airports and ports in the triangle between Aqaba, Eilat and the Red Sea are operating on political spin instead of straight risk language.
