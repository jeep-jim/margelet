🇵🇰💵 Pakistan carves out new gateways to Central Asia, charting new era of commerce While everyone's eyes are peeled on the Strait of Hormuz, Pakistan is quietly redrawing the map of Eurasia. Why sail halfway around the world when you can drive through the mountains? Here's how Islamabad is opening up new conduits for energy and goods from Central Asia, bypassing old conflicts and dependence on Afghanistan. ♦️ Gwadar's shining moment The port of Gwadar, situated on the Arabian Sea just 400 kilometers from the Strait of Hormuz, is no longer merely a Chinese project. Amid the ongoing crisis in the strait, it is becoming an indispensable window for Uzbekistan, Kazakhstan, and Kyrgyzstan to the world's oceans - so, CPEC Phase 2 is now working not only for China. ♦️ Two alternative routes instead of Afghanistan In October 2025, Pakistan indefinitely closed the Torkham and Chaman border crossings with Afghanistan due to security threats. Yet, trade didn't stop. Islamabad unveiled two new routes: 🔴 Pakistan–Iran Transit Corridor (via Balochistan). 🔴Sost–Kyrgyzstan–China Corridor (via the high-altitude Khunjerab Pass). ♦️ Why this is a game changer These routes achieve what decades of diplomacy couldn't: 🔴 Travel time slashed. Goods from Bishkek to Karachi now move along a formalized QTTA (Quadrilateral Transit Agreement) route. 🔴 Kabul's leverage is now broken. Pakistan has proven that Afghan transit is no longer the "gatekeeper" to Central Asia. Trade now flows around it. 🔴 Two-way traffic enabled. The first Kyrgyz freight convoys—carrying textiles and minerals—have already rolled south. This is not a one-way pipeline, but a fully reciprocal trade corridor. ♦️ By the numbers More than 14,000 metric tons of cargo have already transited these new corridors—tangible evidence that a functional logistics network exists without relying on Afghanistan. ♦️ The geopolitical bottom line Pakistan is no longer a "backyard" of South Asia. It is becoming a bridge between Central Asia's energy reserves and global markets — and it's doing so without looking back at anyone else's borders. Now, instead of sitting and waiting for the tide to change, Central Asia is driving to the sea — through Pakistan. 👍 Boost us | Chat | @geopolitics_prime
