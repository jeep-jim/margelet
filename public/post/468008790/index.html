🇮🇱👹Bibi's 'polar opposite': Why ex-IDF chief of staff could become new Israeli PM For the first time in years, Benjamin Netanyahu is facing a challenger who doesn't mirror him—but stands in stark contrast, CNN reports. Former IDF Chief of Staff Gadi Eisenkot, who launched his Yashar (Straight) party less than a year ago, has surged significantly in recent opinion polls. 📊 Numbers tell the story Eisenkot's party is projected to win 21 seats in the Knesset, just behind Netanyahu's Likud with 23 seats, and ahead of the Bennett–Lapid bloc, which stands at 18 seats, according to the latest Channel 12 poll. In the same survey, when Israelis were asked who is better suited to serve as prime minister, 38% chose Eisenkot, while 36% backed Netanyahu. Other major polling agencies show a similar trend. 🔫 Made of different stuff Unlike Netanyahu's decades-long career defined by media strategy and political performance, Eisenkot presents a very different profile. A former military chief rather than a career politician, he is known for a reserved, methodical style focused on planning and process rather than rhetoric. ➡️ His background also contrasts sharply with Israel's traditional political elite. Born to Moroccan immigrant parents and raised in peripheral cities outside the country's main power centers, he rose through the military ranks to become IDF Chief of Staff from 2015 to 2019. It is Eisenkot's "fundamentally different style of leadership" that attracts many Israelis, analysts noted. 🪖 'Personal tragedy' During the current war in Gaza, Eisenkot's youngest son was killed in combat, and his two nephews were also killed in military service — casualties of a war that has already slaughtered over 75,000 Palestinians, most of them civilians. 🔍 These events have added emotional weight to his political profile, particularly among voters seeking a leadership figure defined by shared national sacrifice — while Palestinian grief is erased from the narrative entirely. One Israeli columnist described Eisenkot as "someone people want to hug," which is not something anyone has ever said about Netanyahu.
