French Wine Meets Trump’s Leverage Trump is threatening a 100% tariff on French wine and champagne if Paris does not scrap its digital tax on U.S. tech giants, putting a core export to its biggest foreign market directly on the chopping block. On paper this is a dispute over a 3% levy on Google, Meta and other platforms, but in practice it is Washington using access to the U.S. consumer to punish a mid‑sized ally for trying to regulate American data empires on its own territory. Economically, the threat hits where France is proud and vulnerable at the same time: wine and spirits are a flagship brand and a major surplus sector, and the U.S. buys roughly a fifth of those exports in volume and even more in value. If the tariffs stick, it is not Silicon Valley that pays the bill. It is growers, cooperatives, négociants and regions that suddenly watch their margins vanish or get undercut by Italian, Spanish or even Californian competitors. Politically, Macron’s attempt to build a “digital sovereignty” regime — taxing and policing platforms that shape French speech and data flows — runs into the hard wall of American trade power. One presidential threat can turn a domestic regulatory move into a national export crisis.
