⏰ OKUMA MOLASI ⏰ The Hawthorne Effect refers to the tendency of individuals to alter their behavior when they know they are being observed. The concept originated from a series of workplace studies conducted at the Hawthorne Works in the 1920s and 1930s. Researchers initially sought to determine how environmental factors, such as lighting, affected productivity. Surprisingly, productivity often improved regardless of the specific changes introduced. Later interpretations suggested that workers responded not to the experimental conditions themselves but to the attention they received from researchers. The awareness of being studied appeared to influence performance. The Hawthorne Effect has important implications for social science, medicine, and organizational research. It highlights the difficulty of measuring human behavior objectively, as the act of observation may itself modify the phenomenon under investigation.
