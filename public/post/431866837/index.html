📌 Liquidity Pools: How passive income can quietly burn your coins Key Takeaways ⏺ Adding coins to a DEX liquidity pool earns you a share of trading fees ⏺ Impermanent Loss occurs when the price of one asset moves faster than the other ⏺ This post explains why just holding coins in your wallet is sometimes better than DeFi farming 1️⃣ Pool Mechanics — You always deposit two assets in a strict 50/50 value ratio, like ETH and USDT. The DEX smart contract continuously shifts this ratio based on what other traders buy or sell. 2️⃣ The Trap — If ETH pumps hard, arbitrageurs will drain the cheaper ETH out of your pool, leaving you with more stablecoins (USDT). As a result, you end up with fewer ETH tokens than you started with. 3️⃣ Why it is Impermanent — The loss remains on paper as long as your assets stay in the pool. If the price of ETH returns to the exact level it was when you deposited, the loss vanishes. 4️⃣ Real Loss — If you withdraw your funds while the price gap is wide, the loss becomes permanent. Frequently, this deficit is much larger than all the trading fees you collected. 5️⃣ Practice — To minimize risk, start with pools made of two stablecoins (like USDT/USDC) or highly correlated pairs (like WBTC/BTC). Impermanent loss there is practically non-existent. Final Thoughts Liquidity pool yield is your compensation for the risk of being left holding the weaker asset. Before rushing into farming, check if a simple buy-and-hold strategy serves you better. Subscribe to Crypto Noob for more insights into DeFi traps and yields! #DeFi #Crypto
