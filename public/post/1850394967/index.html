A Letter to Members of the United States Congress Dear Member of Congress, I was born in Iran, but today I am a proud citizen of the United States of America. One of my earliest memories from school is learning a poem by the great Persian poet Saadi. It has stayed with me throughout my life: “Human beings are members of one another, Created from the same essence.” As a child, I did not fully understand the depth of those words. Yet, just as a mother teaches her child their first words, this poem became my first lesson in humanity. It taught me that no person’s suffering is separate from our own. As I grew older, I witnessed inequality and division in my homeland. I dreamed of living in a society where the rule of law prevailed over power, where freedom and human dignity were cherished, and where people were judged not by their nationality, religion, or beliefs, but by their shared humanity. That dream led me to America. I studied the history of this nation and learned that the United States paid a tremendous price to build and protect its democracy, Constitution, and freedoms. When I arrived here, I was inspired by its great universities, research institutions, hospitals, libraries, and the opportunities it offered for education, innovation, and human progress. Paying taxes was never a burden to me. It was a responsibility I accepted with pride because I believed my tax dollars were helping build stronger schools, better healthcare, scientific research, safer communities, and a brighter future. The day I took the Oath of Allegiance as an American citizen, I made that promise with all my heart. I believed that if this country were ever attacked unjustly, it would be my duty to stand with it, because defending justice is also defending humanity. Today, however, I find myself asking a painful question. Should the taxes I faithfully pay be used to build a better future—or to destroy someone else’s? I do not want my tax dollars to finance the bombing of hospitals, schools, universities, scientific institutions, or civilian infrastructure. I do not want my tax dollars to contribute to the deaths of innocent children, patients, civilians, or American and Iranian service members. I do not want my tax dollars to spread war, destruction, and hatred. War destroys far more than buildings. It destroys hope, families, economies, and the future of entire generations. No mother—whether in America, Iran, or anywhere else in the world—should have to bury her child because peaceful alternatives were not fully pursued. As someone who knows both Iran and America, I respectfully ask you to do everything in your power to prevent the expansion of war. Please uphold Congress’s constitutional responsibility over decisions involving the use of military force. Support diplomacy, dialogue, and peaceful solutions whenever possible. Let the taxes paid by the American people be invested in education, healthcare, scientific research, infrastructure, public safety, and the well-being of future generations—not in more destruction. I am still that child who first learned Saadi’s timeless lesson. I still believe that hospitals should heal, not become targets. Universities should educate those who will improve humanity, not become ruins. Children, no matter where they are born, deserve to grow up with hope instead of fear. Saadi’s words remain as true today as they were centuries ago: “Human beings are members of one another, Created from the same essence.” My hope is that these words will be more than beautiful poetry—that they will help guide the decisions that shape the lives of millions. Respectfully,
