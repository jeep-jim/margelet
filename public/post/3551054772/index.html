The AG Nomination on a Layaway Plan Todd Blanche wants to be attorney general, and Donald Trump has found the cleanest workaround yet for two Republican senators standing in the way: just wait for them to leave office. "I have no objection to temporarily withdrawing Todd's name, if they do not do the right thing, and putting him back after Cornyn and Tillis are out of office," Trump posted, naming Sens. John Cornyn and Thom Tillis, both retiring, as the obstacles, The Washington Post reports. It's less a nomination strategy than a countdown clock. What Cornyn and Tillis actually want in writing is almost quaint: proof that the president isn't getting a better tax deal than everyone else. The underlying settlement, resolving Trump's lawsuit against the IRS over his leaked returns, includes a nearly $1.8 billion taxpayer-funded payout pool and blanket tax protections shielding Trump, his family, and their businesses from past claims. A federal judge already called the whole arrangement an attempt to "manipulate" the court, since both sides of the "lawsuit" answer to the same boss. Cornyn puts the ask plainly: "This is about making sure that even if you're the president of the United States, you don't get favorable terms from the government in the settlement of a lawsuit that ordinary citizens would not get." Trump's own framing gives the game away. He didn't dispute the senators' concerns, he dismissed the senators, noting he "refused to Endorse" either of them and that their "political careers have been ended by my action." Translation: their objections don't need answering, just outlasting. Cornyn isn't buying the innocent-bystander act either, telling MS NOW he suspects someone close to Trump is whispering "don't agree to those terms." His retort to the president: he "is mistaken if he believes concerns about the provisions in his tax lawsuit settlement are limited to me and Senator Tillis." So the sitting president is publicly floating a plan to shelve his own attorney general nominee until the two senators auditing his tax settlement are gone, and calling that patience rather than what it actually looks like: waiting out the only people currently positioned to ask real questions.
