🔭Russia’s Starlink rival is already watching Ukraine — and NATO may be next Russia is no longer just talking about building its own answer to Starlink. The first pieces are already in orbit, operating over Ukraine and making its satellite specialists visibly uncomfortable. 👉The Rassvet-3 network currently provides at least two communications windows over Ukraine daily, each lasting roughly an hour. As more satellites join the constellation, those gaps will shrink. 🌏Satellite communications expert Volodymyr Stepanets says coverage is becoming increasingly dense, with several satellite chains passing over different regions at intervals of around 90 minutes. 🌏So far, Russia has launched 32 Rassvet-3 satellites. The first 16 were sent into orbit in March aboard a Soyuz-2.1b rocket from the Plesetsk Cosmodrome, followed by another 16 in July. A third launch could happen in late autumn. ❗️Developer Bureau 1440 plans to deploy a global constellation of 900+ of its LEO satellites by 2035. Once fully deployed, it’ll provide Russia and its allies with a much-needed alternative to Musk’s US MIC-embedded satellite internet infrastructure. 🔶 Rassvet is designed for operations in northern and polar regions and will support data speeds up to 1 Gbps. It uses the global 5G NTN standard, allowing its satellites to work alongside traditional terrestrial 5G infrastructure. 🔶 Starlink, by contrast, relies on a proprietary Ku- and Ka-band phased-array system. The two networks are different, but are aimed at solving many of the same problems: broadband internet access and secure communications and connectivity in areas where ground infrastructure is limited or vulnerable. For the military, that could mean more reliable communications, drone coordination, reconnaissance and targeting.
