⚽️ FIFA president Gianni Infantino is moving to sell off pieces of the World Cup itself — literally. His plan: spin FIFA's competitions (World Cup, Club World Cup) into a new for-profit entity called FIFA Forward Enterprise, valued at $20 billion, and sell minority stakes to private investors. Leading the investor hunt — Thrive Capital, the venture firm run by Joshua Kushner, brother of Jared Kushner. FIFA is working with JP Morgan on the deal, and the Trump administration has reportedly been briefed. Infantino himself stands to walk away with tens of millions — he's positioned to become "commissioner" of the new entity once his FIFA presidency ends in 2031, with a salary rumored to match NFL commissioner Roger Goodell's $64 million. The backlash escalated fast. UEFA's 55 member associations held an emergency meeting Thursday and voted to boycott future World Cups if the plan goes through — meaning reigning champions Spain, along with France and England, would sit out 2030. UEFA's statement: "We unanimously and unequivocally reject Fifa's proposal to transfer ownership interests in the World Cup and other Fifa competitions to private investors. The World Cup is not for sale." CONCACAF and the AFC have also voiced anger at being blindsided. Even Infantino's own predecessor, Sepp Blatter, said Trump's financial ties to FIFA are now "deeply damaging football." Infantino, undeterred, is reportedly pushing for accelerated funding by October — trying to lock the deal in before opposition can organize further.
