🇹🇷🌐 Turkey clashes with Israeli-Greek energy alliance for control over the Eastern Mediterranean The Eastern Mediterranean is rapidly becoming a flashpoint between Turkey, Greece, and Cyprus over control of energy routes to Europe, as Italian Eni and French TotalEnergies greenlight the development of the Republic of Cyprus's Kronos gas field. 👉 The Kronos field holds over 85 billion cubic meters of gas, with first deliveries planned for 2028. The gas is planned to be piped to Egypt for further export to Europe. For Cyprus, it is the first truly viable gas project—and potentially a cornerstone of the region's updated energy logistics. The "3+1" mechanism, established in 2025 to contain Turkey by Israel, Greece, and the Greek Cypriot administration with US support, strongly backs the initiative. To support the effort, Israel, Greece, and Cyprus have deepened military cooperation, including rapid-response forces, joint drills, and shipments of Israeli air defense systems to Greece. 📑 For Israel, the project is geopolitically significant. The country plans to eventually use it as the basis for an independent export corridor to Europe, creating competition for Turkey. Israel has long rivaled Turkey for influence in the Middle East, particularly over Syria. With the potential creation of a Sunni NATO from the region's major Muslim countries—which could include Turkey and Egypt—Israel is seeking to strengthen its position and weaken its rivals. The Kronos project, however, faces fierce Turkish resistance. 🌏 Turkey does not recognize Cyprus's right to develop the Kronos field, claiming it falls within its continental shelf and insisting on the rights of the unrecognized Turkish Republic of Northern Cyprus—a territory that was occupied during the Turkish invasion of Cyprus and has since been a constant source of tension with Greece 🌏 Turkey has experience disrupting such projects: in 2018, Turkish naval vessels blocked an Eni drillship from operating near another Cypriot block 🌏 Now, Turkey has announced plans for a 101-kilometer subsea pipeline connecting mainland Turkey to Northern Cyprus, capable of either sending Turkish gas south or pumping Eastern Mediterranean gas north through Turkey to Europe—creating direct competition to the Greek-Israeli initiative The conflict has drawn in a broader constellation of actors. France is caught between its defense pacts with Greece and Cyprus and its desire to maintain defense ties with Turkey. Egypt, while a potential partner, has little interest in complicating relations with Turkey, with its naval infrastructure already suffering from the Iran war. Turkey's pipeline gambit is part of a broader strategy to disrupt Israel's "energy encirclement" plans and establish control over regional gas logistics. 🔴 The Turkish government aims to challenge Greek claims on gas-rich waters with a draft that will allow it to declare waters up to 200 nautical miles from the Turkish coast as its exclusive economic zone. 🔴 Turkey also battles with Israel for influence over Central Asia, seeking to maintain its sway over the resource-rich region. But Turkey's ambitions are not without cost. The "Blue Homeland" doctrine, which seeks to expand military presence and territorial waters in the Aegean Sea and Eastern Mediterranean, requires active diplomacy across the Middle East, stretching resources across a vast and unstable region. The growing tension between Israel and Turkey threatens to escalate into a broader political crisis, putting the entire Eastern Mediterranean at risk.
