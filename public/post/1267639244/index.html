After five weeks and 103 matches, the 2026 World Cup reaches its absolute climax at MetLife Stadium as an impenetrable Spain takes on a relentlessly offensive Argentina. This final pits two highly contrasting styles against one another: La Roja’s historic, iron-clad defense against La Albiceleste’s high-octane attack led by the legendary Lionel Messi. Spain seeks its second World Cup crown, riding a remarkable 37-match unbeaten streak and setting unprecedented defensive records in North America. ​ Conversely, Argentina hunts for sheer immortality, aiming to become the first nation to win four consecutive major tournaments and successfully defend their 2022 title. With incredibly fine margins defining championship football, this transatlantic clash promises unparalleled drama. Will Spain’s supreme tactical structure stifle South American grit, or will Argentina’s attacking firepower overwhelm the European champions? ​ Spain head into the final with only minor late-week concerns and, crucially, no major selection crisis. Lamine Yamal briefly triggered alarm after missing a recent training session and being seen with a bandage on his left leg, but Luis de la Fuente has confirmed that his teenage star will be in “perfect physical condition.” Pedro Porro is also fit after overcoming muscular fatigue. That means Spain can approach the final from a position of stability, and the likely outcome is continuity rather than experimentation. ​ Argentina’s picture is similarly positive in pure fitness terms. Cristian Romero and Leandro Paredes both recovered from post-quarter-final cramps to start the semifinal against England, leaving Lionel Scaloni with a fully fit squad. The bigger issue is not absences but choices. Scaloni has live competition at right back, in midfield, and in attack, with Gonzalo Montiel, Rodrigo De Paul, and Lautaro Martínez all pushing hard for starting places. In a final of such fine margins, those calls could shape the rhythm of the game. ​ Spain’s strongest asset entering this final may be clarity. De la Fuente has settled on a structure that protects the back line, controls central spaces, and still gives enough freedom to creators higher up the pitch. Yamal’s fitness is a major boost because even when Spain’s defensive metrics dominate the conversation, his presence changes how opponents defend. Porro’s availability matters too after his semifinal contribution, and Álex Baena appears to have established himself ahead of Nico Williams, whose summer has been disrupted by fitness issues. In midfield, Fabián Ruiz is pushing Pedri out of the XI on recent form, and that choice reflects Spain’s preference for balance and continuity. ​ Argentina have fewer injury worries than strategic questions. De Paul looks set to return after starting the semifinal on the bench, and that would restore a more familiar blend of aggression, work rate, and support around Messi. At right back, Montiel is challenging Molina after the latter struggled in the semifinal. Up front, Lautaro Martínez has increased the pressure on Julián Alvarez after scoring the winner against England and reaching three tournament goals, but the expectation remains that Alvarez gets the nod alongside Messi.
