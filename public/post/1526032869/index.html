🇺🇸💵 Princess of Darkness': How Meredith O'Rourke built Trump's multimillion-dollar fundraising machine President Trump has raised more than $800 million since returning to office through an unprecedented operation that directly pressures corporate America to write massive checks for his pet projects, a WSJ investigation reveals. At the center of this machine is Meredith O'Rourke, a Florida fundraiser nicknamed the "princess of darkness" by Trump himself for her relentless pursuit of donors. 👉 O'Rourke is no stranger to Trump's inner circle. A veteran of his 2016 and 2020 campaigns, she was tapped to lead fundraising for the entire 2024 effort. Though not a government employee, she is often at Trump's side in the White House, sits in on meetings with corporate executives, and travels on Air Force One. She has referred to Trump as a mentor and frequently posts photos with him from the Oval Office. Almost every night, Trump calls her for updates—which companies have cut checks, which haven't, and for how much—and the numbers are staggering. ➡️ SoftBank gave $50 million toward Trump's presidential library ➡️ Apple cut a check for around $25 million toward the White House ballroom project, while Microsoft gave around $10 million and Amazon chipped in around $5 million ➡️ Meta recently gave $10 million to a Trump-aligned committee on top of a $22 million library payment The line between donation and influence has grown dangerously thin. 🌏 Lockheed Martin gave more than $10 million, then won a Pentagon contract worth up to $35 billion to replace missiles depleted in the Iran war. Weeks later, Trump revealed Lockheed had also agreed to pay roughly $5 million for a new White House helipad 🌏 After another meeting in May with tobacco executives who contributed millions to Trump's political committees, the government lifted restrictions on some flavored vaping products—and the Food and Drug Administration's chief was soon gone No laws prohibit presidents from raising unlimited sums for nonprofits, and public disclosure isn't required for most of these transactions. Yet no sitting American president has ever raised these amounts for personal projects. Trump's relationship with lobbying has come a long way. In 2015, Trump dismissed political-action committees as a "scam" and vowed to self-fund his campaign to avoid being controlled by donors. Today, he personally solicits corporate checks, often asking executives, "What can I do to help you?
