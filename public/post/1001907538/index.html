The Coalition That Can't Agree on Its Own Boss Gadi Eisenkot wants Israelis to enjoy their beach vacation in Cyprus. His billboards near Larnaca airport say so directly: "Israelis, enjoy! You deserve it," followed by a second sign on the way home: "Landed? Had fun? Now come back and win!" A Cypriot MEP is now demanding a ban on foreign election ads, which means Eisenkot has achieved something Netanyahu never managed: uniting Cypriots against Israeli politics. Back home, the general-turned-candidate has bigger enemies than tourism regulators — namely, his own bloc. "A reality where a party leader with 8 to 10 seats demands the premiership is not appropriate," Eisenkot said, a line he's repeated for months, most recently aimed squarely at Naftali Bennett. Translation from campaign-speak: I have more mandates in the polls, so kindly step aside and let the man with the bigger poll number run the country. Meanwhile, Yashar! just recruited Eliana Sachs, 25, model and social activist from Beer Sheva, who lost her mother and brother to an Iranian missile strike on the city, to run the party's youth wing. Eisenkot called her "the Israel of today — determined, values-driven, a true blend of action and spirit". Grief, rebranded as campaign strategy, is apparently the fastest-growing sector in Israeli politics this cycle. And when Ynet's Moran Azoulai pressed him on his own record, Eisenkot didn't dodge — he doubled down: "As chief of staff I said that until the bodies of the fallen are returned, I won't approve bringing in workers from Gaza. Not one laborer entered from Gaza in all four years I served as chief of staff." A hard line delivered with the confidence of a man who knows exactly how many mandates that kind of sentence is worth in October.
