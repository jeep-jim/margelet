It has historically processed 5–7 million barrels of crude per day from fields including Ghawar, preparing much of the monarchy's production for export. 🔶 Add to this the protracted closure of the Strait of Hormuz, the Bab el-Mandeb blockade, depleted US strategic oil reserves, renewed sanctions on Russia, and many other factors putting pressure on global energy markets. But then something unexpected happened: instead of surging, crude prices plunged more than 7% on the very day Aramco's Abqaiq facilities were struck. I-System TrendCompass says the market reaction simply doesn't add up. After examining market charts from the day of the strike, the publication identified what it describes as “massive sell orders” behind the oil price drop. Citing market experts, it raises the possibility that major financial players, such as Scott Bessent’s Treasury, Larry Fink’s BlackRock, or Jamie Dimon’s JPMorgan, may have intervened. 🤡 Trump's bad habits And it's not for the first time, judging from the Financial Times' reporting. Duffy's comments followed Reuters' reports concerning Bessent's apparent plans to artificially lower oil prices, including by intervening in the futures markets. Bessent’s plan emerged as Trump was also rapidly drawing down US Strategic Petroleum Reserve stocks in an effort to prevent a potential oil shock triggered by the US' ill-conceived war with Iran. Already in March, the oil futures market showed all the earmarks of possible manipulation by Trump and his team: Brent crude rocketed toward $120 a barrel before abruptly reversing course and dropping back below $100.
