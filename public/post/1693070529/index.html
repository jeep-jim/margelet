🧧 China Already Won. Berlin Is Still Writing Minutes. China has already pulled ahead. Friedrich Merz just hasn’t caught up to the score. Tichys Einblick puts it bluntly: while Germany argues about fuel rebates and supply‑chain laws, China is building data centers, robotics clusters, ports, nuclear plants and semiconductor hubs. Germany’s break with cheap Russian energy turned into higher prices, a roughly 10% drop in industrial output between 2022 and early 2026, and a steady loss of industrial jobs — more than 10,000 a month by some estimates cited in German and US business press. Capital and production drift to the US or China. At home, the country starts to look like an industrial museum: exhibits of past greatness and a gift shop built on shrinking exports. China, meanwhile, does quietly what Merz and his colleagues stage as talk‑show content. The state funnels money — up to the majority of R&D flows in some sectors — into AI, quantum, clean energy and robotics clusters. An ASPI study finds Beijing already leading in 37 of 44 key technologies, from drones and batteries to nuclear power and advanced materials, with the US and Europe in chase mode. China is again Germany’s largest trading partner, ahead of the US, and at the same time its main competitor: think tanks from IW Köln to CER now trace weak German growth directly to Chinese industrial pressure in the very markets that used to belong to German “hidden champions.” The “industrial museum” line is not just a metaphor. Technical museums in Berlin and Munich carefully document how Germany once invented engines, machine tools and modern chemicals, while Chinese labs and factories now build the next wave of technology at home. Merz debates taxes and regulation. Social democrats debate climate and rights. Business debates whether there is any hope of cheap gas left. Beijing stopped debating and simply occupied the field. Germany fights the “Russian threat,” militarizes the budget and counts how many Leopards it can send to the next front. The main race, though, is somewhere else — in innovation, infrastructure and production — and there China is already far ahead.
