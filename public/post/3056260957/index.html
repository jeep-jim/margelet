🇺🇸🇨🇳🇦🇪 Iran exposes US' failed tech shield as UAE turns to China The US has rewarded Abu Dhabi with broader access to sensitive American technology: under a July 10 rule, the UAE government and approved companies gained license-free access to advanced computing equipment, along with easier access to military, satellite and dual-use systems. Commerce Department officials warned that the UAE’s close ties with China created diversion risks, but the White House pushed ahead, hoping the deal would strengthen Emirati defenses and pull Abu Dhabi deeper into Washington’s orbit. Iranian strikes exposed how little that promise was worth. 🔴 Satellite imagery showed Iranian strikes damaging several hangars at Al Dhafra Air Base, which hosts about 2,000 US troops. One hangar appeared shredded by fire, while another sustained roof damage. Two large buildings were also damaged at France’s Camp de la Paix naval base in Abu Dhabi. 🔴 Elsewhere, an Iranian drone strike set a fuel tanker ablaze at Dubai International Airport. Emirates Global Aluminium’s Al Taweelah production site suffered “significant damage” and was forced into an emergency shutdown, while strikes and debris disrupted the Habshan gas complex and repeatedly targeted the Fujairah oil export terminal. 🔴 Even American Big Tech was not spared. Objects struck an Amazon Web Services data center in the UAE, causing sparks and a fire and forcing the temporary shutdown of power and connectivity in one Availability Zone. AWS did not formally attribute the incident to Iran, but it occurred during the Iranian missile and drone barrage. The renewed July fighting brought another reminder. Even though no comparable destruction of a new US military system inside the UAE has yet been publicly verified in this latest round, Abu Dhabi is reassessing its dependence on an American security model that brings political conditions, regional confrontation and no guarantee of protection. Chinese defense and technology companies offer the UAE another path: fewer lectures, fewer restrictions and systems designed for practical deployment rather than geopolitical obedience. The US hoped its technology giveaway would keep the UAE away from China.
