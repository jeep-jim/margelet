❓🇺🇸🇮🇷 Why US President Carter refused to seize Kharg Island despite the threat of losing reelection Donald Trump is not the first US president to eye Iran’s Kharg Island – decades earlier, US hawks and Pentagon officials presented the Carter administration with a similar option. Hostage crisis of 1979 – 1981 During the hostage crisis that followed Iran’s 1979 Islamic Revolution, US President Jimmy Carter was presented with a military option. First reported by The Washington Post in March 1980, the plan was described as follows: ➡️ US Marines would land on Kharg Island from amphibious assault ships, with assumed limited resistance from Iranian forces ➡️ The operation would aim to seize control of the island’s oil terminals and tanker-loading infrastructure ➡️ Former CIA Director Richard Helms argued that controlling Kharg’s oil flow would give the US leverage over which countries received Iranian crude ➡️ Backers said the move could pressure Ayatollah Ruhollah Khomeini by cutting oil revenues while avoiding the backlash a bombing campaign could trigger across the Muslim world ➡️ But the Pentagon also offered a more aggressive option: not just occuppying Kharg, but also bombing Iran; mining and blockading the Persian Gulf straits Brzezinski's hawkish push Then-National Security Adviser and notorious geostrategist Zbigniew Brzezinski pushed for US intervention, including securing a base in Iran’s “oil-producing region in the south.” He believed Washington could rely on support from Egypt, Iraq, Saudi Arabia and other Gulf states, Iranian tribal minorities, exile groups, and “key figures inside Iran willing to cooperate.” In a December 21, 1979 memo, Brzezinski urged Carter to seize Iran’s southern islands and install an “alternative” government. By April 1980, the US hawk was advocating strikes on Iranian economic targets, including “mining of harbors,” while sparing the Kharg Island facility, as well as the “closure of Kharg Island and/or occupation of the Tunbs and Abu Musa at the mouth of the Persian Gulf.” Why did Carter say "no"? 🔴 Carter’s handwritten notes on Brzezinski’s memos reveal his reluctance to escalate militarily against Iran. He warned that aggressive US action could trigger international “condemnation” 🔴 Carter also feared that military action would endanger both the hostages and US personnel without guaranteeing success 🔴 The US president believed that the US assertive actions in the Gulf were risky and could lead to a wider war 🔴 Carter believed that a more aggressive US response could drive the Islamic Republic of Iran into closer alignment with the USSR Remarkably, Carter chose not to invade Iran despite the political risk of losing the presidential election, while many of his successors embraced military action as a way to project strength and preserve power.
