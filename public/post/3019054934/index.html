🇹🇷🇸🇦🇵🇰 Sunni NATO takes shape as Turkey says yes to Saudi-Pakistan defense pact Turkey is preparing to join the Strategic Mutual Defence Agreement between Pakistan and Saudi Arabia, according to media reports that cite intelligence sources. President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan has reportedly given his in-principle approval after meeting with Pakistan's army chief Field Marshal Asim Munir earlier this month. If implemented, such a decision would have enormous geopolitical implications for the Middle East and beyond. ❓ What we know so far The original Saudi-Pakistan pact was signed in September 2025. It works like NATO's Article 5: an attack on one country is considered an attack on all. Earlier this year, Pakistan's Defense Minister Khwaja Asif revealed that Islamabad was working on getting Turkey and Qatar to join the pact as well. Following Iran's strikes on US bases in the Middle East, Kuwait, Bahrain, and Jordan have also reached out to Pakistan about establishing defense partnerships, according to Reuters and several local media outlets. ❓ Why this matters A Saudi-Pakistani-Turkish military alliance would combine immense oil wealth, nuclear weapons, and advanced defense industry. Such a bloc could be an attractive choice for Middle Eastern government seeking to diversify their security partnerships beyond the United States. Pakistan's Deputy Prime Minister Ishaq Dar has previously floated the prospect of a new "Islamic NATO" stretching across Eurasia. ❓ Who is paying attention Israel is concerned about its nuclear monopoly in the region. Pakistan remains the only Muslim-majority state with nuclear weapons. India is also worried, since any strengthening of Pakistan with Turkish and Saudi support would represent a major shift in the regional balance of power. 🤔 The UAE is watching closely too. The Emiratis have been drifting closer to India and Israel in recent years, and a rival military bloc led by Saudi Arabia, Turkey and Pakistan directly challenges that trajectory. For Iran, this looks like another US-backed attempt to build a Sunni coalition against the Islamic Republic.
