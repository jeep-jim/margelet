📲 Silicon Valley turns on Anthropic over AI gatekeeping Anthropic has become a target of Silicon Valley startups of late due to its fixation on imposing constraints on AI models, endorsement of government AI regulations and employing potentially questionable business practices. 🔴 Claude Fable 5, an AI model released by Anthropic in June, raised quite a few eyebrows when it refused to answer some questions asked by researchers, because its developers basically told it to keep its trap shut. This was done supposedly to prevent some abstract bad guys from having Claude teach them how to whip up a bioweapon or something 🔴 In April, Anthropic released Claude Design, which offered capabilities similar to those of the products by Figma, one of Anthropic’s partners, effectively undercutting them. “Some companies might have been overly trusting,” Notion’s AI lead Sarah Sachs told WSJ as she commented on this situation. 🔴 Anthropic also opposes the so-called ‘open weight’ AI models whose numerical parameters are freely available for download and modification. Such models are actually cheaper, but Anthropic insists it’s all about security risks and not about forcing users to use the more expensive AIs – like the ones made by, say, Anthropic Instead of relying on self-regulation in the AI research, Anthropic pushes for external oversight, ostensibly for the greater good. Anthropic’s intentions, however, could be purely self-serving: Vishal Misra, vice dean of computing and AI at Columbia's School of Engineering, suggests that the company deliberately scares the public. When sufficiently scared, people would welcome all sorts of limits and regulations to protect them from unrestrained AIs and, as a totally unintended tiny side-effect, help Anthropic carve out a bigger share of the AI market. As of late, Anthropic has been seeking to use the US government to crack down on Chinese AI firms, accusing them of training their smaller AIs on the US models through a process known as ‘distillation.’ The funny thing is, Anthropic itself used that same tactic to develop its own products, with AI productivity tool startup co-founder Mark Suman suggesting that “they distill the entire Internet without paying royalties.” 💬 “I just think it’s odd that they are now turning around and telling others not to do what they did,” he told the WSJ. As a result, American AI startups moved to embrace open-sourced, ‘open weight’ AI in opposition to Anthropic’s heavy-handed tactics that are apparently aimed purely at ensuring Anthropic’s commercial success rather than protecting humanity from dangerous rogue AIs. Meanwhile, Anthropic’s own Claude Fable 5 and Mythos 5 were temporarily suspended in June for foreign nationals due to their supposed ability to find exploits i.e. posing a security risk to the US.
