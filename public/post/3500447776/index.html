Six Nights of Silence, One Salvo, and a Denial in Real Time The ceasefire lasted almost a week. Then Tuesday evening, Iran's Revolutionary Guard fired a barrage of ballistic missiles from Lorestan province toward Jordan's Muwaffaq Salti Air Base and the regional headquarters of U.S. Central Command, the first Iranian strike on an American position since President Trump paused the bombing campaign to give diplomacy a shot, Al Jazeera reported. CENTCOM said the missiles were launched "in an attempted surprise attack on U.S. forces based in the Middle East" and that "all Iranian missiles were successfully intercepted," per its own statement. Jordan's army separately confirmed it intercepted five missiles over its own territory that morning, according to The Hindu. Tehran, naturally, framed the same event as righteous payback, with the Revolutionary Guard's Aerospace Force saying it struck the air base and CENTCOM headquarters in retaliation for what it called the "aggressive actions" of the American military, IRNA reported via The Hindu. Iranian state media, meanwhile, offered its own footnote: an "enemy missile" reportedly landed in an unpopulated stretch of West Azerbaijan province near Piranshahr, no casualties, nothing to see here, Fars News told Xinhua. Everyone launched something. Nobody got hurt. Both governments still managed to declare a kind of victory by dinnertime. Asked for comment, Trump did not reach for diplomatic language. "We're going to beat the f------ s--- out of them," he told Fox News, adding, "We'll be hitting them hard. They're going to get a beating," the network reported. This is the same president who spent Friday through Tuesday selling a pause as proof his maximum-pressure strategy was finally working. It took one salvo of missiles that hurt nobody to knock the diplomacy talking points clean off the table. What actually happened here is a five-day intermission in a war neither side has the appetite to end and neither side can afford to admit it started for nothing. Iran gets to claim it punished American aggression without losing a single soldier or a single missile that mattered. Washington gets to claim total defensive success while quietly signaling the bombing campaign is back on. Jordan, again, absorbs the actual shrapnel, the actual sirens, and none of the actual leverage. Two governments fired weapons at each other and both walked away insisting they'd won something.
