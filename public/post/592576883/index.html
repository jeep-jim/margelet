⚡️🇧🇷🇵🇾 In a recent speech, Brazilian President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva argued for stronger investment in Brazil's armed forces by referencing the War of the Triple Alliance (1864–1870): "One day Paraguay decided to invade Brazil. It invaded Uruguay and Argentina, and that war, which seemed like nothing, lasted five years." The remarks triggered a diplomatic dispute with Paraguay, which summoned Brazil's ambassador, formally protested the statement and described the war as "a sensitive episode" in the country's national history. Paraguayan Foreign Minister Rubén Ramírez Lezcano stated that "the dignity of Paraguay is not negotiable," while Presidents Santiago Peña and Lula later discussed the matter directly in an effort to contain the tensions. The diplomatic fallout has since intensified. Paraguay has now delivered a formal diplomatic note rejecting Lula's remarks "in their entirety," arguing they present historical events "in a distorted manner." Asunción has also renewed calls for the return of Paraguayan war trophies still held in Brazil, including the historic Cristiano and Presidente López cannons, as well as copies of historical documents related to the conflict, describing the request as part of a broader process of historical reparation. Paraguayan officials added that Brazil's ambassador conveyed Lula had no intention of offending the Paraguayan people, while maintaining the government's formal protest. @medmannews
