Houthis opened a Saudi front. Yemen’s Iran‑backed Houthis say they fired missiles and drones at facilities linked to Saudi Aramco in the Red Sea ports of Jizan and Yanbu, right as Riyadh briefly pushed emergency alerts telling residents in Jazan and Yanbu to seek shelter. Yanbu is now the key outlet for Saudi crude since Hormuz is effectively shut by the US‑Iran war, and Jizan hosts both a port and an Aramco refinery. A Saudi‑led coalition hit back with strikes on Houthi positions and Hodeida, the Red Sea port that’s been the launchpad for years of maritime attacks. At sea, the blockade stops being theory. A Hong Kong‑flagged supertanker, New Champion, chartered by a Chinese buyer to load crude in Yanbu, reached the edge of Bab el‑Mandeb — and U‑turned at near top speed, heading back east rather than risk the strait under Houthi threat. That one pivot shows what happens when Hormuz is choked and Bab el‑Mandeb starts to look lethal: Saudi oil can “still get out,” but only through more expensive, more fragile routes, and some buyers decide they’d rather not try.
