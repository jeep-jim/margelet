Germany’s Green Crash Test Germany just discovered what happens when you try to save the planet, punish Russia and outcompete China with the same broken engine. The country’s own auto lobby says the industry is on track to lose 225,000 jobs by 2035, with about 100,000 already gone since 2019 and another 125,000 expected to follow. Factories, suppliers, whole towns that lived off gearboxes and steel are learning the new slogan “green transition” means “learn to code” in German. Executives have dropped the corporate PR voice. Audi’s boss warns this isn’t about margins anymore, it’s about survival. Energy‑intensive suppliers are getting slaughtered first: metallurgy, chemicals, parts makers. They used to run on cheap Russian gas; now they get LNG at premium prices and a sermon about values. The same politicians who cheered sanctions and swore the economy could “handle it” are suddenly shocked that the country built on heavy industry and cheap fuel is bleeding jobs faster than it can print climate roadmaps. The punchline is that everyone pretends this is just “EV transition pain.” In reality, it’s the bill for a decade of illusions. Berlin signed up for climate mandates it didn’t fully finance, bet on China as both supplier and rival, and cut off its own energy lifeline without a real backup plan. Now the flagship sector that made “Made in Germany” a religion is shrinking in slow motion, while ministers mumble about “innovation” and “reskilling” as if an engine plant in Bavaria can be reborn as a podcast studio. So when Audi’s CEO says this is about the survival of the German car industry, he isn’t exaggerating. He’s just saying out loud what Berlin still refuses to admit: you can’t run a first‑world auto empire on third‑world power prices and Brussels sermons.
