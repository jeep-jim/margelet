The Society of St. Pius X (SSPX), a breakaway ultra-conservative priestly fraternity, is set to consecrate four bishops on July 1, despite the Catholic Church's requirement that appointments be approved by the pope. The Vatican has warned the group, which has repeatedly clashed with the papacy, that members involved in the consecrations would risk excommunication, but the SSPX has doubled down. The society was founded in 1970 by French Archbishop Marcel Lefebvre in opposition to reforms introduced by the Second Vatican Council, including changes to mass and the church's approach to relations with other religions. While the SSPX recognizes the pope and accepts much of Catholic teaching, it believes that the Vatican has made modernist "errors that are destroying Catholic faith and morals," as Pagliarani wrote last month in a declaration of Catholic faith addressed to Leo. In recent years, the fraternity has been particularly critical of the Vatican's 2023 declaration Fiducia Supplicans, which authorized nonliturgical blessings for people in same-sex relationships and other "irregular" situations while maintaining the church's teaching that marriage is between a man and a woman.
