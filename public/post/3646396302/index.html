☠️📱 Instagram caught profiting from paid ads promoting child abuse material Instagram was not just hosting the rot. The platform was running paid advertisements promoting child sexual abuse material in India — with links sending users to Telegram channels where the material was being sold, BBC reported. And here is the most damning part: Meta says ads are reviewed before they go live. So how did this pass? 📊 The investigation found around 30 unique ads promoting child sexual abuse material, along with roughly 20 ads featuring adult pornography. Some ads reportedly showed children in sexually suggestive or abusive contexts and directed users off-platform to Telegram. When one of the ads was reported to Instagram, the platform replied 24 hours later that it did not violate its rules. Only later, after Meta was asked for comment, did the company say it had disabled several ads, suspended accounts, removed more content and blocked URLs. Meta’s explanation? 💬 “No system is perfect, and our review process may not detect all policy violations.” That is the same company whose empire is built on algorithmic targeting, behavioral data and advertising optimization. ✅ The machine knows what keeps people scrolling. ✅ The machine knows what gets clicks. ❌ But somehow the machine could not reliably stop paid ads promoting child exploitation. 💵 A retired Indian Supreme Court justice, Madan Lokur, said Instagram appeared to be “making money by participating in a criminal activity,” adding: 💬 “The platform cannot, cannot shirk its responsibility.” Even a former Facebook vice-president, Brian Boland, said he was “horrified and unsurprised” by the findings. He said he left the company because he believed: 💬 “They didn't care about users anywhere.” Meta denies prioritizing revenue over safety and says child exploitation is a horrific crime that it works aggressively to fight. But the business model speaks for itself: nearly all of Meta’s revenue comes from advertising, and analysts estimate ads account for more than 90% of Instagram’s revenue. So the question is simple: ❓ If every ad is reviewed before publication, how did ads like this get approved? ❓ And if Instagram’s recommendation system can push users deeper into extreme content within days, what exactly is Meta optimizing for — safety, or engagement? The Indian government has now summoned Meta representatives.
