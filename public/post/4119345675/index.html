Why NATO is more fragile than ever NATO Secretary General Mark Rutte insists that planned U.S. troop and equipment reductions won't have an immediate impact. "It's not about where forces are now, it's about who will do what when defense plans are activated," he said. But the cuts are steep: F-16 and F-15E jets drop from 150 to 100, P-8 reconnaissance aircraft from 26 to 15, and all eight refueling tankers previously available to Europe would be withdrawn. The U.S. would also cut one bomber group, an aircraft carrier, and reduce destroyers from 17 to 9. For NATO, this would be disastrous. The U.S. also provides unique capabilities Europe cannot easily replace — intelligence architecture, satellite communications, electronic warfare, and precision strike. But the bigger issue may be trust. As one German lawmaker put it: "NATO's main problem is that, as long as Trump is president, there is no longer any faith that the U.S. would come to the Europeans' aid in an emergency."
