📡 Official urges Iran, Pakistan to move beyond goods trade toward economic partnership 📌 Tehran, IRNA — Vice President of the Iran Chamber of Commerce Hossein Pir-Moazzen has called on Tehran and Islamabad to progress from commodity trade to a broader economic partnership. Speaking at the Iran-Pakistan Business Conference in Islamabad on Wednesday, Pir-Moazzen said Tehran and Islamabad must move beyond mere trade and, by strengthening cooperation, develop a suitable model to enhance their economic relations. ​ He also highlighted the importance of utilizing the port capacities of Chabahar and Karachi in trade. ​ He called for reducing clearance times and facilitating the release of goods, reducing stoppages at border areas, developing cooperation in transportation and corridors, creating a database of private-sector actors from both sides, providing a list of goods that both countries can trade, and ultimately removing tariff and non-tariff barriers. ​ He lauded Iran’s unique industrial position in the region, and stressed the possibility of joint production and investment between Iran and Pakistan. ​ The Iranian and Pakistani private sectors are ready to multiply trade; however, to achieve this, barriers that have existed for years on trade between the two countries must be removed. ​ Pir-Moazzen reiterated the need to resolve banking and money transfer issues. ​ Elsewhere in his remarks, he emphasized the importance of removing barriers in transportation and logistics. ​ Cargo routes between the two countries must become faster, cheaper, and more predictable, he said. ​ Accordingly, developing cooperation between ports, railways, road transport, and especially utilizing the capacities of Chabahar and Karachi, can develop Iran-Pakistan trade, he stated. ​ Pir-Moazzen proposed establishing a dispute resolution and arbitration mechanism between the chambers of commerce of the two countries. ​ Iran can offer significant capacities to Pakistan’s private sector in energy, petrochemicals, food industries, agriculture, technology, and industrial equipment fields, he said. ​ Pakistan also has a large market and can serve as a gateway for Iranian companies to South Asian markets, he stressed. ​ He highlighted Iran’s geographical position to provide access routes for Pakistani companies to the markets of Central Asia, the Caucasus, Iraq, and the Persian Gulf countries. 📲 @Irna_en​
