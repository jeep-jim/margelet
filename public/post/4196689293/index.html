America's Missile Shield for Israel Just Filed a Capacity Warning The general running US military operations in Europe quietly told the Pentagon something remarkable this week: he doesn't have enough ships to keep doing the job he's been doing. Gen. Alexus Grynkewich warned that without another Navy destroyer, he'll have to choose defending the American homeland over defending Israel from incoming ballistic missiles, The Washington Post reports. The Pentagon declined to comment. European Command didn't respond. Nobody at the Israeli Embassy could even be reached to comment on the possibility that their air defense might soon be someone else's afterthought. This isn't a hypothetical resource squeeze, it's a confirmation of what Pentagon assessments already found back in May: American weapons, not Israeli ones, have been doing most of the actual intercepting of Iranian missiles headed toward Israel, with THAAD batteries and naval interceptors carrying the load far more than Israel's own air defenses. The US hasn't just been backing Israel up. It's been the primary contractor, and the contractor is now filing a formal notice that capacity is maxed out. The fleet math tells the story on its own. Two destroyers are in the Mediterranean covering Israel. Three more sit in Rota, Spain, some sidelined by maintenance backlogs from the war's pace. Meanwhile an armada near Iran, two carriers and at least 15 warships, is busy enforcing Trump's blockade of Iranian ports, and another destroyer is in the Red Sea fending off the Houthis. Everyone wants a piece of the same finite Navy, and the Navy is running out of pieces to give. Tom Karako of CSIS summed up the position bluntly: "Being on a wartime footing is going to take a toll in terms of readiness and munitions inventory levels. Now the question is: what's the strategy here?" That question has no answer yet, and it's landing in an election year where Republicans are already nervous that gas and grocery prices from this war will cost them in November. So the arrangement now on the table is this: keep blockading Iran, keep patrolling the Red Sea, and hope Israel doesn't need the cavalry on the same night the homeland does.
