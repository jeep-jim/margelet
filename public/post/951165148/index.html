Europe's Solidarity Has a One-Month Expiration Date Spain says the crisis is over. About 50,000 people crossed into Ceuta since Thursday, more than 48,000 were sent back to Morocco within 48 hours, and by Saturday morning overnight crossings had fallen to zero. The death toll now stands at 67. Spain's response to that number is a 500-metre floating barrier at the Tarajal breakwater, sitting a few dozen centimetres above the water where people drowned trying to climb over it. Interior Minister Fernando Grande-Marlaska used the calm to rehabilitate Morocco's image in real time. "Morocco isn't any threat to Ceuta or the rest of Spain, it's a fully reliable partner," he said, adding there had been no intelligence warning of a mass crossing. Sixty-seven bodies say the intelligence apparatus missed something. He blamed smuggling networks and social media rumors instead, which is true and also conveniently spares everyone from asking harder questions about warning signs. The real spectacle, though, is happening between EU capitals, not at the fence. Twenty-two EU governments demanded an emergency meeting on border security, which reads less like solidarity and more like a formal complaint against Spain. Italy didn't wait for a meeting. It just suspended Schengen with Spain for a month. Sanchez fired back on social media: "In the current international context, the EU cannot afford this kind of selfish, polarising and unlawful reaction." Meanwhile Spain, of all EU states, has spent the past year granting legal status to over half a million undocumented migrants already living there, a policy it insists had nothing to do with tens of thousands of people rushing a fence in the same window. So the bloc that prides itself on open internal borders just watched one member unilaterally wall off another over a crisis at the actual external border, the one Frontex exists to help with. Sixty-seven people are dead, Rome didn't bother responding to a request for comment, and the EU's answer to a humanitarian emergency is a continental shrug followed by a punitive travel ban between two of its own members.
