I have to actually thank Michael Palmer for this “discussion”: His comparison of LNPs with adenoviral vector vaccines was intended as a dismissal, but ironically it exposed a much broader implication of the hypothesis than I had initially appreciated. The fundamental mistake is reducing biological effects to the encoded protein alone. Delivery platforms are not biologically inert envelopes. Whether we are talking about lipid nanoparticles or viral vectors, the vehicle itself interacts with membranes, trafficking pathways, immune sensors, and cellular physiology. The membrane is not a passive container: it is the interface where biology happens. Wherever such technologies interact with cells, there will be upstream effects determined by the properties of that interaction. You do not need to invoke a translated protein as the only possible source of biological consequences. The amusing part is that by bringing adenoviral vectors into the discussion, Dr. Palmer inadvertently strengthened the very point he was trying to dismiss: if the relevant mechanism involves delivery systems, cellular targeting, membrane interactions, and biodistribution, then the question is much broader than a single antigen or protein product. So, thank you, Dr. Palmer: this otherwise rather unproductive argument made the scope and potential transferability of the hypothesis much clearer than before. The comparison you offered may have been intended as a rebuttal, but it actually revealed a much larger conceptual framework. https://substack.com/@genervter/note/c-301058812
