⚡️ Night 13: “Free Navigation” by Carpet Bombing At 6:45 p.m. Eastern, the thirteenth consecutive wave of US strikes on Iran began. By morning, CENTCOM declared the cycle “complete.” Between those two sentences, a string of cities that barely existed in previous summaries turned into coordinates: Ahvaz, Andimeshk, Omidieh, Khorramshahr, Kishm, Bandar Abbas, Jask, Konarak, Khorramabad, Khondab, Taft, Shirkuh, Firuzabad. In Ahvaz, local reporting spoke of dozens of strikes in half an hour, more than 30 blasts, hit ammunition depots, four dead, five wounded, and the 92nd Armored Division under bombardment, along with IRGC Basij base “Hamid” nearby. In Bandar Abbas, the lights went out. CENTCOM wrote it differently. In its release, the targets were “military command centers, drone storage facilities, communications networks, coastal observation sites and maritime capabilities,” all “to further reduce Iran’s threat to civilian mariners and commercial vessels transiting the Strait of Hormuz.” In the same breath, the command insisted that “the international waterway remains open” and that commercial ships “continue to sail freely with US military escort” — while more than 50,000 American service members are deployed “to keep the strait open” that ships now cross only with fear and insurance attached. The tools were heavy. B‑1 bombers lifted off from Europe carrying 2,000‑pound bombs — roughly 900 kilos — while dozens of transport and refueling aircraft moved east, and fighter traffic over Baghdad stood out against the Iraqi night. Around Tehran, air‑defense batteries fired in several sectors, and an opposition journalist claimed the drones over the capital were scouting only: “No strike was carried out, it was purely surveillance and intelligence gathering. Very soon there will be the main, very very large strike on Tehran.” Air‑defense systems reveal themselves when they shoot, then the next phase begins. On the Iranian scoreboard, victory looks smaller and louder. One Tomahawk cruise missile reportedly intercepted over Kerman, and an American aircraft “downed” over Kishm island — maybe a big plane, maybe an F‑15, maybe a drone. CENTCOM didn’t respond. Meanwhile, satellite imagery shows Iran rebuilding what was smashed: missile bases dug deep into mountains north of Kermanshah, bridges, ports, factories, roads patched by a single truck. Every night, the US burns more precision munitions to prove control. Every day, Iran proves that shrapnel infrastructure can be put back together fast enough to keep the war going. One necessary footnote: part of the “Ahvaz strike” videos circulating overnight were shot in Gaza. One of the posters admitted it openly and apologized. In a conflict sold as live‑streamed transparency, even the proof of impact is recycled from a different war. So Night 13 looks like this. Strategic bombers from Europe drop nearly‑ton warheads on cities most Americans have never heard of.
