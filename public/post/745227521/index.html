What we need in our schools right now are more caring adults and more human connection,” she said. She said teachers were not anti-technology, but rather pro-child, and take positions based on whether something is beneficial for students. “Putting a lifeless machine in front of students is a bad idea,” she said. “I would say a machine that the intention of it is to simulate a human relationship with young people who are just learning those skills of how to interact, how to be with their peers, how to solve conflicts, and putting a robot that looks like a human that will lead to the dehumanization of the profession of education, is exactly the wrong thing for us to be doing.” Alyson Brown, a community health specialist and local resident and Seneca nation member who graduated from Salamanca, said several students have connections to the traumas of Native American boarding schools. “Many students have connections to relatives who attended the Thomas Indian school located on our Cattaraugus territory.
