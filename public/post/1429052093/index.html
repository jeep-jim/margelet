🇪🇸 The regional government of Spain’s Aragón has announced plans to exclude illegal immigrants from receiving the Aragón Complementary Minimum Income Benefit (PACIMV). Vice President Alejandro Nolasco (VOX) said the government will reform the law to make legal residence in Spain a mandatory requirement for receiving the benefit, arguing the measure is based on a principle of "national priority." Nolasco said the current 2021 law allows an illegal immigrant who has been registered as a resident for one year to qualify for public assistance, which can approach €1,000 per month. According to the Aragón government: - Around 1,100 foreign nationals receive the benefit, at an annual cost of €4.5 million. - Foreign nationals make up 15% of Aragón's population but account for 25% of recipients of the regional minimum income benefit. - 4,682 households receive the PACIMV supplement, while 29 households receive the full payment of just over €800. The proposed reform would also tighten eligibility requirements by requiring a "real, lasting and verifiable" connection to Aragón, including a longer minimum period of municipal registration and Social Security contributions. The measure still requires legislative approval before taking effect.
