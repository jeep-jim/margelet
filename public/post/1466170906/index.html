🕊 A Turning Point: Talks With Iran Are Taking Place Today President Donald Trump said that talks with Iran are taking place today, but declined to set a deadline for reaching an agreement. He previously stated that he had canceled an imminent military strike in hopes of quickly securing a deal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz and resolve the standoff over Tehran's nuclear capabilities. For its part, Iran said it is close to reaching an agreement with Oman on a new route through the Strait of Hormuz. The announcement comes after an oil tanker reported an explosion nearby on Sunday, underscoring the continued volatility of the strategic waterway. The United Kingdom Maritime Trade Operations (UKMTO) said that both the vessel and its crew were safe. Maritime tracking data showed that traffic through the strait slowed on Monday following reports of attacks on commercial shipping. Further south, however, two tankers carrying Saudi oil successfully passed through the Bab el-Mandeb Strait despite threats by the Houthis against oil exports in the Red Sea. Iranian Foreign Ministry spokesman Esmaeil Baqaei told state television that Tehran and Oman were close to agreeing on a new maritime route. “We will now reach an agreement on a route acceptable to both sides — neither the northern route nor the southern route — but one that respects the sovereign rights of both sides and protects our national interests and security.” However, he stressed that such an agreement would not mean the full reopening of the Strait of Hormuz and that it “will in no way return to the status it had before February 28,” the day the war began. On Saturday, President Trump wrote on social media that Iran and other Middle Eastern countries had requested additional time to finalize an agreement that would lead to the “immediate, complete and total reopening” of the Strait of Hormuz and “the end of the Iranian nuclear threat.” He urged Tehran to reach an agreement quickly. Speaking aboard Air Force One on Sunday, Trump said he decided against ordering new US strikes on Iran after consultations with Gulf allies, including Qatar, the United Arab Emirates, and Saudi Arabia, as well as a phone call with Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman. “I said to the Crown Prince, ‘What would you prefer? Would you rather we did that or not?’” “They said they would much prefer an agreement rather than an attack, because you don't know where these attacks lead.” Trump has repeatedly warned that the conflict could escalate, while simultaneously allowing more time for diplomacy. His latest decision marked another shift after several days of threats from both sides. The conflict has spread from the Persian Gulf to the Red Sea and even affected parts of Egypt. Iran largely closed the Strait of Hormuz — a route through which around 20% of the world's oil and liquefied natural gas normally passes — contributing to higher energy prices and broader inflationary pressure. Following Trump's comments, Brent crude fell by more than 4% during the opening of Asian trading, dropping to around $83 per barrel. Trump has argued that preventing Iran from acquiring nuclear weapons justifies short-term increases in fuel prices, but the economic impact has increased political pressure to find a diplomatic solution. Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi also held telephone talks with Saudi Foreign Minister Prince Faisal bin Farhan and Pakistan's Army Chief Asim Munir regarding diplomatic efforts, according to Iranian state media. Israeli Energy Minister Eli Cohen said Israel and the United States maintain close security and intelligence coordination regarding developments across the region. He added: “With or without an agreement, and regardless of external commitments, if Iran tries to revive its nuclear program or advance its ballistic missile industries, we will be there.
