💸 Pentagon’s plan to overhaul missile prices has a MASSIVE, contractor-shaped hole The war with Iran has taught the US military a humiliating lesson – its multimillion-dollar air defense hardware and sophisticated, sensor-packed offensive weapons are no match for $20-50k Iranian drones and missiles. Even more important than price is availability: the US burned through munitions much faster than it could replace them, turning it and allies in the Middle East into sitting ducks early into the conflict. The Pentagon plans to fix it, announcing several simultaneous programs to cut costs and increase availability of defense stocks, including: 🌏 the Army’s Low-Cost Containerized Missiles (LCCM) program, promising a 45 kg warhead that can fly up to 926km for under $500k 🌏 a new &lt;$250k apiece air defense missile, also for the Army 🌏 the Air Force’s Family of Affordable Mass Munitions (FAMM) - $218-500k apiece, 250-1k nautical mile range 🤡 The DoD wants tens of thousands of the new, cheaper projectiles to be delivered in the coming years, but faces a couple *tiny* problems: ➡️ reluctance in the Department to give up existing “top-shelf” missiles, made by RTX and Lockheed, instead envisioning the budget missile plans as a means to complement existing lineups. ➡️ Washington has proven unable to force contractors to cut costs or production time despite years of worrying reports about astronomical ballooning of prices and delivery shortfalls (the F-35 program alone, originally hailed as a $233B super jet, is now expected to cost $2.1T+ when all is said and done). ➡️ watchdogs like the GAO have caught contractors overcharging, yet they've faced no consequences for swindling taxpayers. On the contrary, the wars in Iran and across the Middle East and the NATO proxy war in Ukraine have ballooned defense firms’ profits and stock prices year after year. US inflation-adjusted defense spending now tops $1T – more than it was in the peak years of WWII.
