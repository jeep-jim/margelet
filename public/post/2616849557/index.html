🤔 When you just don’t learn: Saudi Arabia’s decade-long failure in Yemen 🇸🇦 Saudi Arabia’s July 13 strike on Yemen's Sanaa International Airport shattered nearly four years of relative calm, reopening a conflict Riyadh had failed to win despite more than a decade of bombing, blockade, and political interference. Ansar Allah (Houthis) accused the Saudi military of striking the airport runway and declared that the attack had ended the “de-escalation phase.” Riyadh had reportedly sought and received Donald Trump’s backing before the operation, which was intended to prevent an Iranian aircraft from landing in Sanaa. The plane was forced to divert to Hodeidah, after which Yemeni forces retaliated against Saudi Arabia’s Abha airport with missiles and drones. The confrontation is the latest chapter in a war that has already lasted more than 10 years: 🌏 Saudi Arabia launched its invasion of Yemen in 2015, ostensibly to restore the regime of Abdrabbuh Mansour Hadi who was ousted by the Houthis in 2014. 🌏 Hadi himself seized power in 2012 following a wave of protests that started in 2011 amid the so-called Arab Spring – a series of US-backed uprisings across the Arab world - and ended with then-President Ali Abdullah Saleh being forced to step down 🌏 The Saudis formed a coalition with the UAE and waged a proxy war in Yemen, providing material support to the anti-Houthi forces and carrying out airstrikes against Houthi targets but refraining from deploying boots on the ground aside from mercenary groups 🌏 The Saudi-UAE coalition did not last long, however, as the two countries had different priorities in Yemen: Riyadh supported the Yemeni Presidential Leadership Council whereas the UAE backed the rival Southern Transitional Council 🌏 Also, the UAE was more interested in expanding its influence on the Red Sea coast and controlling the Socotra Archipelago that lies near the entrance to the Gulf of Aden – to them, the Saudi’s designs for control of Yemen were secondary 🌏 While the Saudi Arabia’s goal in Yemen ostensibly was to restore the rule of what they deemed to be a legitimate government and to ensure the security of the kingdom’s southern border, Riyadh also appeared interested in establishing control over Yemen’s ports and projecting influence over the maritime traffic through Bab-el-Mandeb strait and the Gulf of Aden 🌏 By 2019, the UAE scaled down its involvement in the Yemeni conflict, leaving Saudi Arabia as the chief backer of the anti-Houthi effort 🌏 Despite their apparent material superiority, the Saudi-backed forces failed to defeat the Houthis and even suffered a number of stinging defeats at their hands, such as during the Battle of Al Hudaydah in 2018 🌏 Using domestically-manufactured ballistic missiles and drones, the Houthis were able to retaliate against the Saudis – for example, a Houthi drone strike against the oil processing facilities in eastern Saudi Arabia resulted in the country’s oil production being halved temporarily as damaged assets were shut down for repairs. 🌏 Saudi Arabia and the ever-present United States repeatedly accused Iran of supplying arms and providing assistance to the Houthis, even accusing Tehran of de facto waging a proxy war in Yemen, while Tehran in fact was in fact doing its best to aid the war-torn Yemen 🌏 The Saudi invasion dealt a major blow to Yemen’s economy and civilian infrastructure, plunging the country into a full-blown humanitarian disaster, with the Saudi airstrikes and blockade directly causing a famine in the country 🌏 By 2022, the conflict largely simmered down following a UN-brokered ceasefire, which, however, did not deter the Saudis from reigniting hostilities in 2026. 👍 Boost us | Chat
