Hormuz Strait Opens on Schedule — Trump's Schedule The Strait of Hormuz has spent 2026 in a permanent state of "opening tomorrow." Tuesday brought the latest installment: Trump told Fox News the U.S. is "having very good discussions" with Iran, then promised Tehran would get "hit very hard" if the strait didn't reopen "very soon" — presenting a signed deal and a bombing campaign as basically interchangeable outcomes. A day earlier, in the Oval Office: "You'll find out today or tomorrow... they're going to go quickly one way or the other," with the strait possibly "literally by tomorrow, completely open." Phase one of two. Phase two, denuclearization, "will take a little while." Tomorrow has been open since April, when Oman and Iran were first reported to be working out a toll for tankers. The war itself started February 28. This time there is paper. Axios reports a draft: inbound traffic through a northern lane in Iranian waters, outbound through a southern lane in Omani waters, no tolls for sixty days, mines cleared from the median lane within thirty. Real terms, real deadlines, an announcement targeted for today. Which is exactly what everyone had three weeks ago. Mediators thought the deal was done, Iran went back to hitting ships, and the two weeks of fighting that followed came close to a full war. The draft is not evidence that this ends. It is evidence that drafts are cheap. Tehran, meanwhile, denies negotiating with Washington at all — the talks are with Oman, and its foreign ministry says the agreed route will be neither the northern one nor the southern one. The two sides are not disputing terms. They are describing different maps. Last Friday the Revolutionary Guard announced it had struck two tankers crossing the strait under American air escort and turned four others back. Not a leak, not an accusation from Washington — a press release from Tehran. Iran advertises attacks on shipping while denying it is at the table, and expects both statements to be believed at once. In May, asked whether the midterms were shaping his patience with Iran, Trump said he didn't care about them. Three months on, with November closing in, the conflict polling badly for Republicans and fuel prices doing the damage, he is calling off strikes on Saturday to protect a draft and demanding the announcement by Wednesday. That is not the behavior of a man with time. The only participant with no reason to posture is the price. U.S. crude is down more than 10% on the week and sliding toward $75, a third consecutive session lower — the market has read the draft and believes it. It believed the last one too, in June, when Brent fell back to pre-war levels.
