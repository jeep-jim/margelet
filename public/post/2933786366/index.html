Trump’s Holy Deal: Bomb First, Bill Later Trump didn’t “flip” on Iran. He just moved from phase one — outsourced bombing — to phase two: cashing in on the ruins. In this logic, Israel plays the attack dog, Gulf monarchies pay for reconstruction, and America sits on top like an East India Company with better branding. None of these texts bothered to explain how to end a war — because endless pressure is the model. Exit strategy is not a bug. It’s heresy. Inside the US right, two religions wrestle over the joystick. The dispensationalist crowd wants Israel to “take it all” for the glory of the End Times — for them, any deal with Iran is betrayal. The America First nationalists don’t care about prophecy; they care that Iran is defanged, trade routes are secured, and the US can declare victory without dying in another forever war. Trump plays to both: green‑lights strikes, then signs a deal once Tehran is weaker and the logistics map looks promising. For Iran, the subtext is worse: once your nukes and regional muscle are knocked down, Washington prefers you alive, sanctioned and manageable — a neutered state that signs away its program so America can say, “Problem solved,” retroactively justifying what its own doctrine wrote years earlier. In that sense, Trump’s Iran deal isn’t a glitch in his hawkish persona. It’s the point. Use delegated violence to break the threat, then freeze the new reality in place with a signature and a payment plan.
