🇨🇳 China has taken another step toward making its semiconductor industry independent from foreign suppliers. A Shanghai-based company has begun production of immersion DUV lithography machines. Five units are expected to be built this year, and by 2027 production is set to increase to around 20 units. This is a move that will help mitigate the impact of Western sanctions. The first recipients of the Chinese systems are set to be SMIC, CXMT, and Hua Hong. The biggest unknown remains the capabilities of the new machines. It is not yet known what their throughput is or what advanced process nodes they can handle. For comparison, the latest TWINSCAN NXT:2150i from the Dutch company can process over 300 wafers per hour, but the Chinese offering will certainly be less efficient initially. For CXMT, which produces DRAM memory using older process nodes, this may not be a serious limitation. A much more difficult task awaits SMIC, which may try to use the equipment to produce 5nm-class chips using multi-patterning. Nevertheless, even if the first Chinese machines prove significantly weaker than ASML's systems, their launch carries major strategic significance. Beijing gains a foundation for further developing its own lithography and gradually reducing the impact of Western sanctions. @medmannews
