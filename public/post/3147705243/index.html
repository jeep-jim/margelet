🌐🎛 How Google and Wall Street are underwriting Anthropic’s $200B chip binge Google has built one of the most convoluted financing machines to keep Anthropic swimming in AI chips that the start-up could never buy on its own credit, reports the Financial Times, citing insiders and corporate filings. 👉 Google, Broadcom, Apollo, Blackstone, Morgan Stanley and a slew of crypto-mining companies are now tied into a web of deals, private-credit vehicles, residual-value guarantees and data-center backstops reportedly totaling ≈ $200bn. Anthropic has no credit rating and no proven ability to generate the cash needed for hardware at this scale. Yet it is being handed tens of billions in TPUs (Tensor Processing Units) - specialized computer chips built specifically to do AI work more efficiently than regular processors. Here’s how the machine works: 🔴 Google designs the TPUs (its answer to Nvidia chips) and wants to sell them externally in large “pods” Broadcom helps make the chips and formally commits to buy them from Google — but does not want the hardware sitting on its own balance sheet 🔴 A special-purpose vehicle (a shell company created just for this), funded mainly by private-credit giants Apollo and Blackstone and arranged with help from Morgan Stanley, buys the chips and leases them to Anthropic 🔴 Broadcom provides a residual-value backstop on the senior debt: if Anthropic stops paying and the used chips sell for less than expected, Broadcom covers most of the shortfall for the senior lenders 🔴 Separately, Google guarantees the data-center leases so that former crypto miners can raise cheap construction debt to house the chips The first $35bn tranche - roughly one million TPUs, or about 1GW of capacity - has already moved through this structure. A much larger commitment of another 3.5GW sits behind Broadcom’s $128bn of disclosed purchase obligations. Google has already backstopped multiple data-center projects, and is racing to secure enough power for the full 4.5GW of TPUs already agreed. Buying the chips was only half the battle. Google also needed data centers with enough power to run them. Crypto miners with spare capacity stepped in. Data centers backed by Google are borrowing money at 7.1% interest, while Nvidia’s rivals have to borrow at 9.3% - something that Wall Street analysts call "a structural disadvantage" for everyone else.
