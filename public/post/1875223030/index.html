🌏 🇮🇷 How Iran is expanding its geopolitical contest with the US and Israel beyond Middle East Iran is not sitting on the sidelines as Gulf states, Israel and their Western allies extend their influence across Africa, Latin America and Asia – the Islamic Republic is playing its own geopolitical game. Africa Iran is accelerating its African outreach: after decades of ties, it made the continent a foreign policy priority in 2024 and convened three Iran-Africa summits between 2023 and 2025, attracting around 40 countries. 🔴 Northern Africa Alongside Algeria, Iran seeks to counter growing US-Israeli influence in Morocco, opposing Rabat’s entry into the Abraham Accords and rejecting its claims over Western Sahara. Reports have also pointed to Iranian support for the Polisario Front, the movement seeking independence for Western Sahara through the self-declared Sahrawi Arab Democratic Republic (SADR) 🔴 Eastern Africa Iran has reportedly backed President Abdel Fattah al-Burhan and the Sudanese Armed Forces (SAF), including via the supply of Mohajer-6 drones between December 2023 and January 2024. Notably, the SAF also enjoys Saudi support, while its rival, the Rapid Support Forces (RSF), is backed by the UAE – deeply allied with Israel After reviving ties with Kenya, Uganda and Zimbabwe in 2023, Iran formalized its long-standing military technical relationship with Ethiopia through a 2025 security and intelligence-sharing deal. Even as Ethiopia works with the UAE, it has kept its partnership with Iran on a separate track 🔴 Sahel As Mali, Burkina Faso, Niger and Chad curtailed Western military presence between 2022 and 2025, Iran moved to expand its footprint in the Sahel through offers of military cooperation, energy and scientific assistance. Iran's deepening ties with Niger, formalized through 2025 security and cooperation pacts, rattled the US as it lost its military foothold in the African country in 2024. Latin America As the Trump administration and Israel move to curb pro-independence governments in Latin America through overt pressure and alleged electoral fraud, Iran's relations with Venezuela, Argentina and Bolivia have become strained. Nonetheless, it still maintains close ties with Brazil, Nicaragua and Cuba. Iran's Latin American pivot is driven by resistance to US pressure and the pursuit of multipolarity Asia 🔴 China serves as Iran's primary economic lifeline, purchasing the vast majority of its oil exports, while India is involved in its major infrastructure projects such as the Chabahar Port and the International North-South Transport Corridor 🔴 Iran and North Korea maintain a decades-long strategic partnership rooted in their shared resistance to the West 🔴 Iran has also consolidated relations with Kazakhstan, Uzbekistan, Turkmenistan, Kyrgyzstan, and especially Tajikistan, which it sees as a brotherly nation.
