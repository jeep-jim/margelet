🔥 Night 12: Hormuz as Reality Show On the twelfth straight night, CENTCOM pressed “repeat.” At 5:30 p.m. Eastern, another wave of US strikes rolled over Iran’s coast, sold with the same copy‑paste line as every night before: protecting “civilian mariners and commercial vessels” in regional shipping lanes. By morning, the target list read like a demolition tour — naval assets, missile and drone storage, coastal surveillance sites, air defenses — wrapped in a promo slogan about “more than 50,000 American service members” staying “alert, focused, lethal and most ready” across the Middle East. The map of hits filled in overnight: Bandar Abbas, Ramshir in Khuzestan, Bushehr, Sirik port, Kharg, Ahvaz, the islands of Kishm and Qeshm, Tabriz, Zanjan, Kermanshah province for the first time since Operation Roaring Lion, Jask, Konarak and Mahshahr. Khuzestan’s deputy governor reported missile strikes near Ahvaz; Bushehr’s governor confirmed attacks in the city and said a power station was hit by a US missile near the nuclear plant, a detail echoed by Arab media. Local agencies added another wave on a military site. At the Shalamcheh crossing, likely aimed at an Iranian border‑guard HQ, officials admitted two deaths. Opposition channels spoke of more, naming one victim as Amir Mohammad Salami of the Revolutionary Guards’ signals corps and noting that Iran’s national police chief, Ahmad‑Reza Radan, had visited the crossing hours before the strike. For the first time since fighting resumed 12 days ago, long‑range B‑1B bombers from British bases were used against IRGC targets — even as US briefers insisted that bombers weren’t flown “last night,” and official footage from the south showed little more than a pier, a shovel and a boat. On the other side of the water, reports from Jask spoke of wounded and strikes on IRGC navy bases; state TV literally asked civilians to shoot down enemy drones and helicopters themselves. The Guards announced that no tanker would pass Hormuz without their coordination, claimed two tankers had turned back and one had caught fire, and later there were reports of explosions on Kishm island and in the strait, plus a missile hitting its shore. Meanwhile, IRGC units handed out shoulder‑launched Misagh heat‑seeking missiles — Chinese‑based SK‑7s with a range of up to six kilometers — to Basij activists and junior personnel. The army that runs advanced air‑defense batteries is now asking its citizens to point second‑hand launchers at the sky and hope. So the twelfth night looks like this. The world’s biggest navy carpet‑bombs “maritime threats” with strategic bombers. The regime that closed Hormuz orders villagers to shoot drones with export knock‑offs. Between them, 2,000 ships and tens of thousands of sailors sit stuck on both sides of a strait that used to be an energy corridor and is now a live‑fire exhibition. Security is the word both sides use.
