The Strategy of Blinding: Why Iran Is Keeping Israel Out of the Fight — For Now By researcher A. There is a fascinating paradox in the way Iran is managing the current escalation in the Middle East. While it is setting the region ablaze, it also appears to be making a deliberate effort to avoid drawing Israel directly into the conflict. This is likely neither accidental nor the result of deterrence, but rather a calculated strategic decision. To understand Iran’s approach, one must understand the difference between the campaign being fought now and the one Iran is actually preparing for. The Existential War vs. the Tactical Campaign Iran’s current successes are based on applying pressure to Arab states and to the American military presence in the region. Its objective is to wear down its opponents, extract concessions, reshape the regional balance of deterrence, and force the West into compromises or withdrawals. Israel, however, is a different story. If Iran were to attack Israel directly, it would lose this indirect leverage and enter an entirely different arena. Israel does not fight limited engagements merely to reach a negotiated settlement. If Israel enters a full-scale war, it does so from the perspective of an existential conflict. Israel has a structured, precise, and comprehensive war plan. It would not seek a symbolic victory leading to a ceasefire on Iranian terms, but would instead focus on systematically destroying military capabilities. Iran understands this well. That is why, at least for now, Israel remains outside the equation. The Trap for Israel But this is precisely where the danger lies. The fact that Iran currently seeks to avoid a direct confrontation with Israel does not mean it is not preparing the battlefield for the day when such a confrontation becomes unavoidable. Iran’s actions throughout the Middle East reflect a familiar operational concept: to “blind” Israel by gradually stripping away its regional layers of defense, so that when the decisive moment arrives, Israel will stand increasingly isolated and be forced to rely almost exclusively on its own defensive systems. This pattern is not new. For years, studies—including research conducted by the Jerusalem Center for Security and Foreign Affairs (formerly JCPA) under the leadership of Brig. Gen. (Res.) Yossi Kuperwasser—have described Hezbollah’s doctrine of systematically targeting the “eyes” of the IDF: radars, surveillance systems, and intelligence collection assets, in order to degrade Israel’s ability to detect launches and gather real-time intelligence. Iran is now applying the same concept—but on a regional scale. First, it targets regional radar and early-warning systems through attacks on American bases. Second, it seeks to weaken the American defensive umbrella. Recent footage suggests that American interceptor batteries have struggled—and in some cases failed—to cope with large missile barrages. Third, it employs missiles with hypersonic characteristics, alongside cruise missiles and drones, with increasingly accurate strikes against defensive systems themselves. Preparing Israel to Stand Alone Iran may not want Israel to enter the war today, but it is certainly working to ensure that when the day comes, Israel will face it largely alone. As the region’s defensive layers erode, Gulf radar systems are degraded, and American interception capabilities are weakened, the burden increasingly shifts to Israel’s own air-defense network—Arrow, David’s Sling, and Iron Dome. These are outstanding systems, among the best in the world. Yet, as past experience has demonstrated, even they cannot provide absolute protection against massive barrages of fast, highly accurate missiles launched simultaneously from multiple fronts. It is therefore essential not to mistake Iran’s current reluctance to confront Israel directly for genuine deterrence. The relative quiet Israel currently enjoys on the direct front is not a gift. It is time that Iran is using to systematically erode the defensive shield surrounding Israel.
