✈️ Trump’s Luxury Jet, Discount Security Donald Trump wanted a new, glamorous Air Force One in time for America’s 250th birthday — and he got it by cutting corners on the one thing the plane is supposed to guarantee: survival. Instead of waiting for the fully hardened new 747‑8s under conversion, Trump fell in love with a Qatari royal 747‑8 — chandelier, wood paneling, suede lounge — and ordered it rammed into service as a “bridge” Air Force One on an eight‑month schedule. The Times investigation shows that the Qatari jet was not brought up to the old Air Force One standard. It has no lower fuselage door with self‑contained stairs, so the president now relies more on local ground equipment in unsecured airports. It carries only one auxiliary power unit instead of two, reducing backup power for communications and vital electronics. And the visible infrared countermeasure pods — the laser‑based systems that track and jam incoming missiles — are missing from the usual positions on the wings and tail, even as officials refuse to say what, if anything, replaces them. Work that normally takes years — cutting new doors into the fuselage, electromagnetic hardening of wiring against nuclear‑type pulses, deep repairs to known 747‑8 frame cracks, crash‑testing side‑facing seats — was compressed to fit Trump’s July 4, 2026 deadline. A sitting president personally signed off on décor, paint scheme, and the level of defensive systems, while appropriators were told the details were a “national security secret” they couldn’t see. FAA approvals for the unusual seating arrived just days before his first flight, and the Air Force itself admits it “made trades on some of the less commonly used mission sets,” a polite way of saying certain capabilities were dropped to hit the date. When US‑Iran strikes resumed and intelligence picked up a credible threat from Iranian proxies against Trump’s aircraft in Ankara, the Secret Service insisted he leave Turkey on the old, fully hardened Air Force One instead. The new plane was flown ahead to Britain for a troop photo op.
