☢️ Israel’s Nuclear Apocalypse™: 30 Years of “Any Minute Now” Historical fact: since the early 1990s, Benjamin Netanyahu has been promising that Iran will get the bomb “within 3–5 years.” From his 1992 Knesset speech to his 1995 book, from the cartoon bomb at the UN to endless interviews, the deadline keeps rolling while reality stubbornly refuses to cooperate. Israeli assessments swing from “by the end of the year Iran will have enough uranium for one bomb” in 2020 to “after the 2025 strikes Tehran has been pushed back two to three years,” but Bibi’s script never changes: “very soon, any day now.” Campaign fact: in June 2025, the US and Israel hit Fordow, Natanz and Isfahan. Trump stood in front of cameras and declared that Iran’s “nuclear sites are totally and completely destroyed,” that the threat was over — “obliteration” as official branding. Months later, US intel and media reports politely walked that back: the program as a whole survived, the clock was set back by months, maybe a year, and significant enriched stock and centrifuges were moved or hidden. Before long, familiar estimates reappeared: Iran is roughly 9–12 months from a weapon, if it chooses. Today’s intel line is boringly precise: Iran hasn’t made a formal decision to build a bomb, but it has the technical ability to get there in less than a year, even after airstrikes. Netanyahu’s rhetoric lives in a different universe: it ignores three decades of missed “red lines” and insists that every new report means disaster is days away — until the next assessment pushes the bomb back to “a few more months.” 🇮🇷 “Mount Pickaxe” and the Engineering of Fear After the June 2025 war, one thing became obvious: the “destroyed” sites were just the visible layer. According to satellite analysis and Western reporting, Iran has been moving thousands of centrifuges into tunnels under so‑called “Mount Pickaxe” near Natanz and hardening its program deeper into the Zagros rock. Israeli and Western estimates now say Tehran’s breakout time remains around 9–12 months. The capability exists; the political decision does not. Netanyahu’s system doesn’t know what to do with that ambiguity. For 30‑plus years he has sold the same storyline: Iran is “on the verge,” “months away,” “approaching the point of no return.” Trump, by contrast, tried to declare the story over: “we reduced Iran’s nuclear project to zero, the threat is gone,” a marketing campaign to book the war as a clean win. A year later, Mount Pickaxe, missing enriched uranium and fresh intel estimates quietly resurrect the mantra: the program lives, the timeline is back. The result is institutionalized paranoia. Israeli political discourse ping‑pongs between “we destroyed everything” and “we are one minute from annihilation.” Mount Pickaxe is not just an engineering project; it’s a metaphor. The real program is buried deep, adjusting and surviving.
