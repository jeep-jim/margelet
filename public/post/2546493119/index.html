⏰ OKUMA MOLASI ⏰ The Pygmalion Effect is a psychological phenomenon in which higher expectations lead to improved performance. The concept gained prominence after studies suggested that when teachers believed certain students had exceptional potential, those students often showed greater academic progress over time. Importantly, the students were not necessarily more capable at the outset; rather, the expectations of others appeared to influence outcomes. Researchers propose that expectations can subtly shape behavior. Teachers who anticipate success may provide more encouragement, feedback, and opportunities, creating conditions that foster improvement. Similar patterns have been observed in workplaces, sports teams, and military organizations. The Pygmalion Effect demonstrates that performance is influenced not only by ability but also by social context. It highlights how beliefs and expectations can become self fulfilling, affecting achievement in ways that are often difficult to recognize.
