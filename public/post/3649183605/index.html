🇺🇸🚢 US pushes ahead with new submarine deal despite contractor delays – what's the rush? The Pentagon has reportedly approved a deal with General Dynamics to build nine next-generation Virginia-class attack submarines – even as the company faces mounting problems. 🤔 More orders, same problems General Dynamics has been plagued by persistent delays and cost overruns. The Government Accountability Office found that projected delivery dates for the Block V submarines awarded in 2019 continue to worsen, slipping by an average of almost three years since the original contract award. The crisis is compounded by a workforce shortage: the US shipbuilding industry is short of roughly 250,000 skilled workers it needs to build the submarines already ordered, and training a master welder takes 30 years. So the Pentagon is pushing ahead with more advanced submarines from a contractor already struggling to meet existing orders — hardly an obvious fix for a production bottleneck. Against this backdrop US President Donald Trump reportedly demanded that General Dynamics accelerate submarine construction on July 15, pressing the company to speed up delivery of the delayed Block V boats. The new Block VI contract for an even more advanced design is being pushed forward despite the contractor's poor performance. 🇨🇳 China threat or convenient justification? The urgency becomes clearer once China enters the picture. Virginia-class submarines are central to US plans to maintain an undersea advantage in the Pacific as China expands its navy and strengthens its defenses around its own coastline. But Washington is trying to compete from an increasingly uncomfortable position. 👉 But the numbers are not moving in Washington’s favor. The US operates around 50 attack submarines across several theaters, while China’s fleet is expected to reach about 80 by 2035. Beijing also has a vastly larger shipbuilding base, giving it an industrial advantage that nine more submarines will not erase. Meanwhile, the US is expanding arms support for Taiwan and military cooperation with countries such as the Philippines, while presenting China’s response as justification for an even larger regional presence. So perhaps the real concern in Washington is not simply that China is becoming stronger, but that America’s ability to operate freely in China’s neighborhood can no longer be taken for granted.
