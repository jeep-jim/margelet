🇪🇸 Spain's best firefighting helicopters — grounded by Brussels' own sanctions For a second straight summer, Spain's Kamov Ka-32 fleet sits parked. Ten Russian-built helicopters, each capable of dropping over 5,000 litres of water with a precision the ministry itself admits has no market equivalent — sit unused while the country burns. The problem isn't safety. It's Brussels. The EU's 2024 sanctions on manufacturer Kamov cut off spare parts and barred the Russian technicians required to certify the aircraft airworthy. EASA pulled the Ka-32's flight certificate. Sumar's parliamentary group pushed Madrid to seek an exemption for civil protection equipment. The government's answer: not our call, take it up with Brussels. No exception followed. A €92 million tender to replace the fleet has sat unresolved since January. Meanwhile: 13 dead in the Almería fires, over 100,000 hectares burned by the government's own count — satellite tracking puts it closer to 160,000.
