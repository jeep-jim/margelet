🤖 The AI investment race is not only a technology story It is also a capital-allocation story. A new BIS working paper models the current AI build-out as a race for a small number of dominant market positions. The result is uncomfortable. Under the paper’s conservative baseline, firms overinvest by roughly 50% relative to the socially efficient level. Under weaker demand conditions, that gap can rise to around three times the efficient level. Why? Because every company faces the same pressure: Invest early. Build capacity first. Secure the market before someone else does. That can produce: • duplicated infrastructure • debt-funded expansion • circular financial relationships • specialised assets that are difficult to resell • larger losses if revenue expectations fail The paper does not argue that AI lacks economic value. It argues that real technological value does not guarantee efficient capital allocation. A transformative technology can still create an unstable investment cycle. For markets, the useful question is not only: “How powerful is the technology?” It is also: “How much capital already depends on the most optimistic outcome?” Capability explains the opportunity. The balance sheet explains the fragility. Source: BIS Working Paper No. 1367, The AI Investment Race, 14 July 2026.
