🪖🇺🇸📲 Pentagon wants AI data centers on military bases, while Congress is not having it The US military is pushing to host massive commercial AI data centers on its own installations. But on Capitol Hill, alarms are already sounding: over cybersecurity, strained power grids, water supplies, and the very readiness of American bases. The Pentagon itself won't build or run most of these facilities. Instead, it plans to lease underused land to private firms on long-term deals. Companies would foot the bill for construction, secure their own energy hookups, and manage every layer of infrastructure. Two projects are already in the pipeline. Investment giant Carlyle is eyeing a hyperscale center at Texas's Fort Bliss, on a sprawling 560-hectare site. CyrusOne is being tapped for a similar complex at Utah's Dugway Proving Ground, covering 486 hectares. The Air Force, meanwhile, is scouting bases in California, Arizona, Georgia, New Jersey, Tennessee, and Alaska. 👉 The thing is the Pentagon needs serious computing firepower: for AI training, autonomous systems, drones, and battlefield command networks. And it wouldn't mind backup power and a slice of commercial cloud capacity on the side. But Congress wants hard rules. One defense-bill amendment would ban Chinese-linked chips, circuit boards, and components from any base hosting these centers. Lawmakers also demand strict cyber-risk and counterintelligence reviews. Yet the real flashpoint isn't even the hardware, but control. A commercial data center inside a military base supercharges capabilities, yes. But it also becomes a glittering target for hackers and saboteurs. And that makes every base that hosts one more vulnerable, not less. 👍Boost us | Chat | @geopolitics_prime
