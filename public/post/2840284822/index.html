🇬🇧UK gambling compliance enters a more expensive and data-driven era 🔵On June 30, the DCMS confirmed a 25% increase across most UK Gambling Commission (UKGC) license and application fees from October 1, 2026, while society lottery fees will remain frozen and illegal-market enforcement will be funded separately through a £26 million Treasury commitment. 🔵At the same time, UKGC confirmed a phased rollout of Financial Risk Assessments (FRA), starting with high-spend customers exceeding £5,000 in net deposits within 24 hours, or £2,500 for younger and higher-risk customers, before lower age-based thresholds apply once fully implemented. Meanwhile, the FRA rollout triggered strong industry pushback, with concerns from Wiggin, a UK-based law firm, focused on credit reference agency data inconsistencies, possible customer friction, revenue impact, and the risk of players moving to unlicensed operators. 💭 Inteliumlaw opinion: Gambling businesses operating in the UK should reflect the 25% fee increase in their 2026-2027 budgets, prepare internal procedures for FRA-triggered customers, align customer interaction policies with the upcoming guidance, and build a clear audit trail to avoid overly defensive decisions that could harm both compliance outcomes and player retention. For new applicants, the higher fee environment should be factored in when planning to enter the market, especially where UK licensing is being compared with alternative regulated jurisdictions. 🗂️ Ready-Made Solutions | 👩‍💻Tailored Legal Solution
