🔻 Exclusive | Ayatollah Khamenei’s legacy extends beyond Iran’s political boundaries, says Italian analyst 📌 London, IRNA – Giulio Chinappi, an Italian political analyst and a researcher from the Center for Mediterranean Eurasia Studies (CeSEM), says the legacy of the martyred Leader of the Islamic Revolution, Ayatollah Seyyed Ali Khamenei, must be understood beyond the boundaries of Iranian politics. In an exclusive note for the IRNA office in London on Wednesday, Chinappi said that martyred Khamenei’s leadership embodied the defense of sovereignty, resistance to imperial pressure, and the conviction that independence remains the foundation of any just international order. ​ He also added that Ayatollah Khamenei’s legacy is inseparable from the issue of Palestine. “For decades, he placed the Palestinian cause at the heart of the moral geography of the Islamic Republic. While many governments in the region normalized occupation, forgot the refugees, or transformed Palestine into an occasional rhetorical issue, Ayatollah Khamenei insisted that Palestine remained the central wound of the Muslim world and of all those committed to justice.” ​ “This commitment shaped Iran’s regional policy and made the Islamic Republic a reference point for forces resisting Israeli occupation and Western domination.” ​ The full text of his memo is as follows: ​ The funeral ceremonies for Ayatollah Ali Khamenei mark a moment of mourning for the Iranian people. They also offer an occasion for historical reflection for those who have followed the development of the Islamic Republic of Iran, the transformation of West Asia, and the long struggle of independent nations against imperial domination. His passing, in the tragic context of the aggression against Iran, closes a major chapter in the history of the Islamic Revolution, but it does not close the political and moral horizon that his leadership helped define. ​ Ayatollah Khamenei belonged to a generation shaped by colonial arrogance, monarchical despotism, religious conviction, anti-imperialist struggle, and the difficult task of building an independent state under continuous external pressure. To understand his legacy, it is necessary to consider this background. He was a religious and revolutionary leader whose authority naturally extended into the political and institutional sphere. His formation took place in opposition to dictatorship and foreign interference, and he later assumed responsibility for maintaining the continuity of the Islamic Republic under sustained external pressure. ​ From the victory of the Islamic Revolution in 1979 to the long decades that followed, the central question facing Iran extended beyond institutional matters. It was civilizational and geopolitical. Could a country of Iran’s size, history, culture, and strategic position remain independent in a world order structured by American hegemony? Could it refuse subordination, defend its own model, support the oppressed peoples of the region, and still survive sanctions, isolation, war, sabotage, and political demonization? Ayatollah Khamenei’s leadership was, in many ways, a long answer to these questions. ​ His answer was based on strategic patience, internal resilience, and resistance. Under his leadership, Iran faced waves of sanctions, repeated military threats, assassinations of scientists and commanders, sabotage campaigns, political destabilization, media warfare, and efforts to isolate the country from the international system. Yet Iran did not collapse. It did not become a client state. It did not surrender its sovereignty.
