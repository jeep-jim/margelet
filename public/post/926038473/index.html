Eleven Icebreakers and a Golden Photo Frame President Trump wanted more icebreakers than Russia has. He didn't consult a strategic review to decide how many. He consulted his own frustration. "We only have one," he complained back in 2020. "Russia has 40." Russia actually has 56 now. China has five. The Coast Guard's own 2023 assessment said the US needed four or five. The number Trump landed on was 11, The New York Times reports. The deal traces back to a golf tournament. Finland's president, Alexander Stubb, played alongside Trump in March 2025 and gave him a photo of an icebreaker in a gold frame. Months later, Trump called Stubb late one night with a bigger ask. "I'm not going to buy the used one, because it's too old," Trump reportedly told him. "I want more new ones instead." Eleven ships, worth almost $7 billion, split between shipyards in Finland and the US. No competitive bidding. A waived law banning foreign-built national security vessels. Coast Guard Commandant Admiral Kevin Lunday put it plainly: "The number 11 came directly from the president. This was the president's number and the president's decision." The public interest exemption meant the Coast Guard never had to prove urgency or justify the number. It just had to send Congress a heads-up letter, after the deal was already announced. Shelby Oakley of the Government Accountability Office wasn't impressed. "We haven't seen any information or analysis to justify the 11 or to assess the long-term affordability of these decisions," she said. She compared the Coast Guard to "a lottery winner who spends all their money buying the mansion, and then goes broke once they realize they don't have the money to maintain it." Nobody has funded the crews or infrastructure to actually operate 11 ships. That bill comes later. Even Senator Ted Cruz, celebrating a Texas shipyard groundbreaking, couldn't quite explain the number with a straight face. He reached for Spinal Tap instead. "As a child of the '80s, I have to admit, my own personal theory is the 'Spinal Tap' theory: Ours goes to 11," he said, then added, "Although I doubt that was the president's motivation." One senator quoting a mockumentary about aging rockers chasing irrelevance through excess, to explain a $7 billion no-bid defense contract.
