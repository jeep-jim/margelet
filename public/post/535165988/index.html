Knesset Inc.: The Closing-Down Sale Before The Vote The Knesset is in recess. Nobody told the money. On Tuesday, the Finance Committee approved 267 million shekels ($88 million) in coalition funds. This happened weeks after the government itself dissolved parliament for the October 27 election. MK Naama Lazimi called it plainly. "Theft from the serving public," she told the committee. That's not a figure of speech. The package includes 26 million shekels to keep yeshiva students from dropping out, 12.5 million for "Haredi culture," and funding for the Vaad HaYeshivot — an organization whose actual track record is helping young men dodge the draft. Somewhere, a reservist is reading this between rotations. The same marathon session added another 113 million shekels for settlement expansion. That pushes this year's settlement budget to a record 821 million shekels. Finance Minister Bezalel Smotrich billed it as funding for "more than 70 archeological and heritage sites". Activists stormed the hearing room. Committee chairman Hanoch Milwidsky had a warm welcome ready: "a mixed multitude, these are worse than [Nukhba]. Disgusting. Repulsive". Comparing protesters to the Hamas unit that carried out the October 7 massacre is one way to change the subject from a budget line item. Hiddush and Lazimi tried the courts instead. They asked the Supreme Court to block the meeting before it started. Justice Alex Stein declined. "I did not see fit to grant interim relief," he ruled. Then he ordered the petitioners to report back by 9 a.m. Wednesday on exactly what got approved. The court can always read the receipt after checkout. Eighty-four days before an election, the recess is decorative, the cash register stays open, and the only oversight in the room is a judge who'll get back to you in the morning.
