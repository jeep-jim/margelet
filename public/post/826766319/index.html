☢️🔍 What if the US or Israel actually nuke Iran? Technically, both the US and Israel possess the military capabilities to deliver such a strike. The US maintains a stockpile of roughly 5,200 warheads – an arsenal spanning yields from modest tactical warheads to city-levelling strategic bombs. Israel, under its policy of ambiguity, is widely estimated to hold dozens to over a hundred warheads. But would either of them be reckless enough to resort to the nuclear option - and would the consequences be worth the cost? ⚛️ Nuclear taboo Since the US atomic bombings of Hiroshima and Nagasaki in 1945, the world has lived with a never codified in law "nuclear taboo." Once broken, there is no guarantee it could ever be restored. 🤔 The ‘limited strike’ myth Supporters of a nuclear option often point to so-called tactical nuclear weapons — smaller warheads designed to strike military targets rather than entire cities. Others suggest an even more limited scenario: detonating a nuclear device in an uninhabited desert as a warning to Iran. But a nuclear blast is still a nuclear blast. ♦️ Even a demonstration explosion would shatter a global norm that has held for decades ♦️ It would send a powerful message to every government that security can no longer be guaranteed through diplomacy, treaties, or international law ♦️ As only nuclear weapons can deter nuclear threats, proliferation would explode ♦️ For Iran, the message would be immediate and irreversible: acquire your own deterrent, at any cost ☢️ Fallout would extend far beyond Iran 🔴 Anti-US sentiment would surge across much of the world 🔴 NATO allies in Europe might fracture under the moral weight of association with nuclear first-use 🔴 Nuclear powers from Russia and China to Pakistan and North Korea would be forced to reassess their own strategic calculations 🇮🇷 There is also a widespread misconception that a single nuclear weapon could simply erase Iran from the map. ♦️ Iran spans 1.6 million square kilometers of rugged mountains, dispersed cities, and strategic depth - one or two bombs cannot “erase” it ♦️ Even Tehran, one of the largest metropolitan areas in the Middle East, could not be completely destroyed by a single bomb ♦️ Fallout, environmental contamination, economic collapse, mass displacement, and regional instability would continue for years History suggests that overwhelming force does not necessarily compel surrender, and more often hardens resistance and strengthens defiance. A nuclear attack might also supercharge Iran’s drive for nuclear armament, while the international order could tip into open nuclear brinkmanship. 👍 Boost us | Chat | @geopolitics_prime
