Free Fire Zone, With No Fire Israel Katz went on camera and delivered the line every defense minister loves: “There was and is no limitation on IDF soldiers in Lebanon to act to remove threats… all of the IDF’s achievements are preserved… Israel will not withdraw from the security zone in Lebanon.” On paper, it’s total freedom of action, zero political interference, full deterrence. In reality, the order that matters went the other way. According to Israeli military reporting, already at the start of last week — before five IDF soldiers were killed and before Trump’s memorandum was signed — Northern Command received a near‑full fire freeze: strikes that used to be cleared at division or regional command level were pushed up to the chief of staff and sometimes the political echelon, “in line with an American demand.” Officers complain that in recent days the IDF is constrained even in areas immediately adjacent to its own ground forces, forced to sit under artillery harassment without the usual air and artillery responses. The numbers make the lie obvious. Since yesterday evening there hasn’t been a single reported Israeli airstrike in southern Lebanon; aside from limited artillery fire directly protecting forces, even the new “yellow line” zone is under tight fire discipline. On the other side of the border, an Al Araby correspondent describes “complete calm” in villages and towns in the south since 6 p.m., noting no strikes and not even artillery fire — a “tense quiet” that locals haven’t heard in months. So both realities run in parallel. For domestic politics and deterrence theater, Katz insists there are “no limits” and “no retreat” from the security zone. On the ground, the IDF operates under a de facto U.S.-brokered cease‑fire, where every bomb needs a signature from the top, and the loudest sound on the Lebanese side of the line is suddenly silence.
