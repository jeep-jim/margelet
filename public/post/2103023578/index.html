🇰🇪🇧🇩Bangladesh sharpens digital enforcement, while Kenya opens a stricter licensing landscape 🇰🇪Kenya launched its first licensing cycle under the Gambling Control Act, 2025, following the publication of 5 new regulations covering licensing, advertising, foreign-based operators, technology standards, and player protection, as per a FocusGN article released on July 8. Online gambling operators in Kenya must now integrate with the regulator’s Central Monitoring System, provide real-time API access, apply geolocation, segregate player funds, and, unless exempted, store and process gambling data in Kenya. 🇧🇩Bangladesh’s Gambling Prevention Act 2026 grants authorities new enforcement powers to block gambling-related websites, applications, and digital platforms, reinforcing the country’s shift toward technology-led gambling control, as reported by SiGMA World on July 8. The new law treats gambling-related laundering through banks, mobile financial services, digital wallets, or crypto as a predicate money laundering offense, with organized schemes facing up to 10 years’ imprisonment and fines of up to BDT 50 million (~$400,000). 💭 Inteliumlaw opinion: These two updates show how fast emerging markets are moving away from light-touch or outdated gambling regimes toward either hard prohibition or intensive licensing supervision. Operators, affiliates, payment providers, and crypto-facing platforms should avoid targeting Bangladeshi users or processing Bangladesh-linked gambling flows to avoid criminal and AML-related risks. For Kenya, existing operators should reapply under the new framework and review supplier authorizations, data-hosting arrangements, and responsible gambling controls before the 60-day transition period expires. 🗂️ Ready-Made Solutions | 👩‍💻Tailored Legal Solution
